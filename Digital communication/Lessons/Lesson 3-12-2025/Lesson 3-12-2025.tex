\end{align}

Previous symbols interfere with current symbols!

This is the IO relationship. If we want to estimate the channel, what do we want to do?
We don't transmit an unknown symbol $\boldsymbol{x}$, but rather some \textbf{known symbols}; this is indicated with $\boldsymbol{p}$, which stands for \textit{pilots}.

\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}

Before diving further, let's address the values which they'll use.
We are indeed sending $N_p$ pilots, but since the channel has $L$ taps, all received symbols up until the $L-1$th will have \textbf{contributions which we don't want.}
In order to remove such contributions \textbf{we only consider the received signals from $L$ to $N_p-1$.}

\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}

Specifically, we have the synthesized form
\begin{align}
[\boldsymbol{y}[L] \quad \dots \quad \boldsymbol{y}[N_p-1]] &= \notag \\
= \sqrt{G} [\boldsymbol{H}[0] \quad \boldsymbol{H}[1] \quad \dots \quad \boldsymbol{H}[L]]
&\begin{bmatrix}
\boldsymbol{p}[L] & \boldsymbol{p}[L+1] & \dots & \boldsymbol{p}[N_p-1] \\
\boldsymbol{p}[L-1] & \boldsymbol{p}[L] & \dots & \boldsymbol{p}[N_p-2] \\
\vdots & \reflectbox{$\ddots$} & \reflectbox{$\ddots$} & \vdots \\
\boldsymbol{p}[0] & \boldsymbol{p}[1] & \dots & \boldsymbol{p}[N_p-L-1]
\end{bmatrix} + \bar{\boldsymbol{V}} \notag \\
\leftrightarrow \bar{\boldsymbol{Y}} = \sqrt{G}\bar{\boldsymbol{H}}\bar{\boldsymbol{P}} + \bar{\boldsymbol{V}}.
\end{align}

Where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item since each pilot is a $N_t \times 1$ vector, the $\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}$ matrix will be a $N_t(L+1) \times (N_p - L)$ matrix.
    \item Since each $\boldsymbol{H}[\ell]$ matrix is a $N_r \times N_t$ matrix, the $\bar{\boldsymbol{H}}$ matrix will be a $N_r \times N_t(L+1)$ matrix.
    \item Both $\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}}, \bar{\boldsymbol{V}}$ will be $N_r \times (N_p - L)$ as a result of the product $\bar{\boldsymbol{H}}\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}$.
\end{itemize}

$N_p$ is the number of pilots we transmit and it's up to us to decide its value.
If we transmit too many pilots, we can't transmit the information symbols.
We need to transmit some pilots (generally a vector of all pilots) and then some pilots scattered around the time and frequency domains.

\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}

After going to our matrix notation, we further progress to a vector notation, knowing that if we were to stack one on the other all the values of $\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}}$, we'd get
\begin{align}
\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}}) &= \sqrt{G} \, \text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{H}}\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}) + \text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{V}}) \notag \\
&= \sqrt{G}(\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}^T \otimes \boldsymbol{I}_{N_r})\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{H}}) + \text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{V}}) \notag \\
&= \sqrt{G}\boldsymbol{P}_{\otimes} \, \text{vec}(\boldsymbol{H}) + \text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{V}}). \tag{2.229}
\end{align}
We can apply the forementioned property; in applying it, we use $\boldsymbol{A} = \boldsymbol{I}_{N_r}$, we can develop like it's written above here.
Question is: what's $\boldsymbol{P}_{\otimes}$? It's just $\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}^T \otimes \boldsymbol{I}_{N_t}$.

\section{Linear estimation for a MIMO AWGN channel}

Now we estimate, either with ML or LS.

In this estimation, \textbf{we assume that we know the conditional distribution of the received signal given the channel.} In other words, we know that our transmit receive relation will follow (2.229) and that our noise $\bar{\boldsymbol{V}}$ will be AWGN.
Thanks to the fact that we can choose our pilots to be \textcolor{green}{IID}, we know that the \textbf{ML estimator will be equivalent to the MAP estimator.}

Considering (2.229), we have that $\boldsymbol{P}_{\otimes}$ is known and that the observation given by a certain channel realization $\mathbf{h}$ (which realizes the distribution of $\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{H}})$) is complex Gaussian with mean
\begin{align}
\mathbb{E}[\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}})] = \mathbb{E}[\sqrt{G}\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}\underbrace{\mathbf{h}}_{\text{realization}} + \text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{V}})] = \sqrt{G}\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}\mathbf{h} + 0
\end{align}
and covariance
\begin{align}
\mathbb{E}[(\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}}) - \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}})})(\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}}) - \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}})})^*] &= \mathbb{E}[\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{V}})\text{vec}^*(\bar{\boldsymbol{V}})] \notag \\
&= N_0 \boldsymbol{I}.
\end{align}

We'll thus have that, given a certain realization of the channel, $\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}})$ is Gaussian with PDF (according to \textit{\textcolor{green}{Uni Generale/Digital Communications/Complex Gaussian distribution > 57a95e}})
\begin{align}
f_{\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}})|\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{H}})}(\boldsymbol{y}|\mathbf{h}) = \frac{1}{(\pi N_0)^{(N_p-L)N_r}} \exp \left( - \frac{\| \boldsymbol{y} - \sqrt{G}\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}\mathbf{h} \|^2}{N_0} \right), \tag{2.230}
\end{align}
which, thanks to the monotonicity of the exponential, is equivalent to minimizing $-\log f_{\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}})|\text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{H}})}(\cdot)$, ultimately giving
\begin{align}
\text{vec}(\hat{\bar{\boldsymbol{H}}})(\mathbf{y}) = \arg \min_{\mathbf{h}} \| \boldsymbol{y} - \sqrt{G}\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}\mathbf{h} \|^2. \tag{2.232}
\end{align}
This is identical to the deduction of the zero forcing equalizer \textcolor{green}{we already discussed} (equations (2.145), (2.146)), ultimately giving
\begin{align}
\text{vec}(\hat{\bar{\boldsymbol{H}}})(\mathbf{y}) = \underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{G}}(\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}^*\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes})^{-1}\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}^*}_{\boldsymbol{W}^{\text{LS}*}}\boldsymbol{y}, \tag{2.233}
\end{align}
and thus the linear \textbf{least squares estimator}
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{W}^{\text{LS}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{G}}\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}(\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}^*\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes})^{-1} \tag{2.233+}
\end{align}
to the observation (we have $^{-1}$ and not $^{\dagger}$ since we know that $\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}^*\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}$ is a square matrix).

From this we do IDFT and we have 256 elements:
\begin{align*}
h = \begin{bmatrix}
\text{stuff}_0 \\
\vdots \\
\text{stuff}_{15} \\
0 \\
\vdots \\
0
\end{bmatrix} \updownarrow 256
\end{align*}

If we don't have zero, it means we have noise; we then just replace everything with zeros and we to the DFT: we have $H^*$, a better version of $\hat{H}$.

Assuming we haven't made a wrong decision on $L$ (we thus must have $L < K$), we can cut everything after $L$, which will be noise.
This is what we mean by saying \textit{we can work in the time domain}.

\subsection*{Example 2.24 (Least-squares estimator for a frequency-flat SISO channel)}

Let $L=0$ and $N_t = N_r = 1$. For this simplest of settings, a single scalar pilot suffices. With such a pilot being $\sqrt{E_s}e^{j\phi}$ where $\phi$ is an arbitrary phase, (2.247) reduces to

\begin{align}
\hat{h} = \frac{e^{-j\phi}}{\sqrt{G}E_s} y \tag{2.249}
\end{align}

while $\boldsymbol{E}$ in (2.239) reduces to

\begin{align}
\text{MMSE} &= \frac{N_0}{G E_s} \tag{2.250} \\
&= \frac{1}{\text{SNR}}, \tag{2.251}
\end{align}

which does not depend on $\phi$; the pilot's phase can thus be set to zero.

For $N_p > 1$, $\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}$ becomes a row vector and the pilot sequence requires no special structure: in this setting, $\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}^*$ is always a scalar, even if the same pilot symbol is simply repeated. It is easy to verify that

\begin{align}
\text{MMSE} = \frac{1}{N_p \text{SNR}}, \tag{2.252}
\end{align}

indicating that the accrual of energy over multiple pilots has the same effect as increasing the energy of a single pilot.

\section*{Example 2.30 (LMMSE estimation for an IID MIMO channel)}

Let the entries of $\bar{\boldsymbol{H}}$ be IID and, by normalization, of unit variance. Then, $\boldsymbol{R}_{\text{vec}(\boldsymbol{H})} = \boldsymbol{I}$ and

\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{W}^{\text{MMSE}} = \sqrt{G} \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes} (N_0 + G \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}^* \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes})^{-1}, \tag{2.270}
\end{align}

from which

\begin{align}
\text{vec}(\hat{\boldsymbol{H}}) = \sqrt{G} \left( G \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}^* \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes} + N_0 \boldsymbol{I}_{(L+1)N_t N_r} \right)^{-1} \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}^* \text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}}), \tag{2.271}
\end{align}

while the MMSE matrix in (2.262) specializes to

\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{E} = \boldsymbol{I} - G \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}^* (G \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes} \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}^* + N_0 \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes}. \tag{2.272}
\end{align}

In the foregoing expression for $\text{vec}(\hat{\boldsymbol{H}})$, the dimensionality of the identity matrix has been noted explicitly to set the stage for the decomposition $N_0 \boldsymbol{I}_{(L+1)N_t N_r} = N_0 \boldsymbol{I}_{(L+1)N_t} \otimes \boldsymbol{I}_{N_r}$. Recalling that $\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\otimes} = \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}^T \otimes \boldsymbol{I}_{N_r}$, we can then write

\begin{align}
\text{vec}(\hat{\boldsymbol{H}}) &= \sqrt{G} \left( G \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}^c \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}^T \otimes \boldsymbol{I}_{N_r} + N_0 \boldsymbol{I}_{(L+1)N_t} \otimes \boldsymbol{I}_{N_r} \right)^{-1} (\bar{\boldsymbol{P}}^c \otimes \boldsymbol{I}_{N_r}) \text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}}) \tag{2.273} \\
&= \left[ \sqrt{G} (G \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}^c \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}^T + N_0 \boldsymbol{I}_{(L+1)N_t})^{-1} \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}^c \otimes \boldsymbol{I}_{N_r} \right] \text{vec}(\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}}) \tag{2.274}
\end{align}

and, resorting once more to the identity $\text{vec}(\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{C}) = (\boldsymbol{C}^T \otimes \boldsymbol{A}) \text{vec}(\boldsymbol{B})$, what ensues is

\begin{align}
\hat{\bar{\boldsymbol{H}}} = \sqrt{G} \bar{\boldsymbol{Y}} \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}^* (G \bar{\boldsymbol{P}} \bar{\boldsymbol{P}}^* + N_0 \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}, \tag{2.275}
\end{align}

which coincides with its least-squares counterpart in (2.247), save for the argument of the inverse being regularized in proportion to the noise strength. This similarity is consistent with the fact that the channel statistics, which differentiate the least-squares and LMMSE estimators, are trivial when the entries of $\bar{\boldsymbol{H}}$ are IID; an SNR-dependent regularization suffices to optimally bias the estimate of each realization. The possibility of unvectorizing the estimate in the form (2.275) is also a consequence of the receive antennas being uncorrelated, as then the LMMSE estimate of each row of $\bar{\boldsymbol{H}}$ can be produced separately, based only on the corresponding row of $\bar{\boldsymbol{Y}}$.
