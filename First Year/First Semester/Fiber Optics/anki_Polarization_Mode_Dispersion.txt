Describe origins, effects and main parameters of PMD.	Polarization Mode Dispersion (PMD) is a signal distortion phenomenon caused by the fact that standard optical fibers are not perfectly cylindrically symmetric. In perfectly symmetric fibers, the two orthogonal polarization states of the fundamental mode would have equal propagation constants. <br>     PMD is caused by factors that introduce asymmetries or stresses into the fiber, breaking the cylindrical symmetry. <br>     The main effect of PMD is Differential Group Delay (DGD). <br>     \begin{itemize} <br>         \item The fiber supports two orthogonal polarization states, called the Principal States of Polarization (PSP), which propagate without distortion. <br>         \item These two states travel at different group velocities, resulting in two replicas of the input signal arriving at different times. <br>         \item These two states travel at different group velocities, resulting in two replicas of the input signal arriving at different times. <br>         \item DGD is a random variable and is proportional to the square root of the fiber length (DGD $\propto \sqrt{L}$). <br>     \end{itemize} <br>     PMD is also characterized by the PMD matrix (Q), which summarizes the effect of polarization on propagation. <br>  <br>     \begin{itemize} <br>         \item The eigenvalues ($\tau_k$) of the PMD matrix correspond to the differential group delays. <br>         \item The eigenvectors ($\hat{v}_k$) of the PMD matrix correspond to the PSPs. <br>     \end{itemize} <br>     PMD is also represented by the PMD vector ($\overline{\Omega}$) on the Poincaré sphere.
What is Polarization Mantaining Fiber (PMF)?	A Polarization Maintaining Fiber (PMF) is a special type of optical fiber where the cylindrical symmetry is deliberately and uniformly broken along the fiber's length. <br>  <br>     This deliberate asymmetry allows for the definition of two distinct eigenstates, or principal states of polarization (PSP), which can propagate through the fiber unchanged. <br>  <br>     In this type of fiber, the magnitude of the PMD vector ($\overline{\Omega}$) is proportional to the fiber length ($L$) ($\overline{\Omega} \propto L$), unlike standard fibers where DGD is proportional to $\sqrt{L}$.
