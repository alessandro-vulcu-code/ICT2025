What are some types of optical amplifiers?	\begin{itemize} <br>         \item Erbium Doped Fiber Amplifiers (EDFA): These use fiber doped with Erbium (Er), which is a rare earth material; <br>         \item Parametric amplifiers: These are noted as being "not mature" but yield the least noise products. <br>         \item Stimulated scattering amplifiers: These exploit scattering phenomena that are intrinsic to the silica material. <br>         \item Semiconductors amplifiers: These use light from some of the III-IV semiconductors, such as gallium arsenide. <br>         \item Invasion population amplifier: This is a type of amplifier that uses fiber doped with Erbium (Er). <br>     \end{itemize}
Describe the light-matter interactions in an EDFA.	Light is made of photons, which carries energy $E = h\nu$. Atoms can have some levels of energy so electrons can jump from one layer to another. <br>     We can analyze three types of interactions: <br>     \begin{itemize} <br>         \item Stimulated absorption: atom may absorb a photon and jumps to a higher energy state. The population changes according to: <br>               \[ \frac{dN_2}{dt} = \sigma_{1,2}\Phi N_1 \qquad \text{with }\begin{cases} <br>                       \sigma_{1,2} = \text{cross section}                         \\ <br>                       \Phi = \text{flux: }\left[(\#\text{photons} / s)/m^2\right] \\ <br>                       N_1 = \text{population of bottom layer} <br>                   \end{cases} \] <br>         \item Spontaneous emission: electrons from the top layer decay generating a photon of energy $h\nu = E_2 - E_1$.\\ <br>               It is the source of noise in amplification. <br>               \[ \frac{dN_2}{dt} = -\frac{1}{\tau_{sp}}N_2 \] <br>         \item Stimulated emission: basically electrons decay generating duplicating the entering photons. In general $\sigma_{1,2} \neq \sigma_{2,1}$ <br>               \[ \frac{dN_2}{dt} = -\sigma_{2,1}\Phi N_2 \qquad \text{with }\begin{cases} <br>                       \sigma_{2,1} = \text{cross section}                         \\ <br>                       \Phi = \text{flux: }\left[(\#\text{photons} / s)/m^2\right] \\ <br>                       N_2 = \text{population of top layer} <br>                   \end{cases} \] <br>  <br>  <br>     \end{itemize}
Explain the three-level system.	Assume we have 3 levels, with many electrons that can move between the levels (given by doping material). <br>     \begin{itemize} <br>         \item From L3 (top) $\to$ L2 (middle): L3 instable, we have a quick decay to L2 <br>         \item L2: Metastable, we need a sufficiently large $\tau_{sp}$ to store the electrons there <br>         \item Pump-photon: $h\nu = E_3 - E_1$, they promote electrons from L1 to L3 <br>     \end{itemize} <br>     If we pump fast enough we make L2 full of electrons, and we reach a condition of \textbf{Population Inversion}, so there is amplification by stimulated emissions.\\
Explain what are the rate equations.	rate equations are needed to analyze the performance of the EDFA.\\ <br>     What does it mean `to pump fast enough'? Suppose we have an infinitesimal cylindre of fiber: <br>     \begin{itemize} <br>         \item surface $s$ <br>         \item length $\Delta z$ <br>         \item in-flux $\Phi(z,t)$ <br>         \item out-flux $\Phi(z+\Delta z, t + \Delta t (=\frac{\Delta z}{c}))$ <br>     \end{itemize} <br>     So a first rate equation is given by \textbf{known in-flux} + \textbf{contribute of stimulated emissions} - \textbf{contribute of stimulated absorption} - \textbf{expected out-flux} = 0 <br>     \[ s\Delta t\left(\phi_s\left(z,t\right) - \phi_s\left(z + \Delta z, t + \frac{\Delta z}{c}\right)\right) + \left(s\Delta z\right)\Delta t\left[\sigma_s \phi_s\left(z,t\right)\left(N_2\left(z,t\right) - N_1\left(z,t\right)\right)\right] = 0 \] <br>     At the end, after Taylor expansions, and knowing that $I_\square = h\nu_\square\phi_\square$: <br>     \[ \text{Pump} : \frac{\partial I_p}{\partial z} + \frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial I_p}{\partial t} =- \sigma N_1I_p \] <br>     \[ \frac{\partial I_s}{\partial z} + \frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial I_s}{\partial t} = \sigma(N_2 - N_1)I_s \] <br>     \textbf{Balance of population density}, we need to consider also spontaneous emissions. Since $N_{tot} = N_1 + N_2$: <br>     \[ \frac{\partial N_2}{\partial t} = \frac{\sigma_p I_p}{h\nu_p}N_1 - \frac{\sigma_s I_s}{h \nu_s}(N_2 - N_1) - \frac{1}{\tau_{sp}}N_p \]
Explain what are the rate equations for 3-level system.	The rate equations for a 3-level system are used to analyze the performance of an Erbium Doped Fiber Amplifier (EDFA). <br>  <br>     When neglecting the time dependence, the rate equations for the signal intensity ($I_S$) and pump intensity ($I_P$) in the stationary regime are: <br>  <br>     Signal Intensity: $\frac{\partial I_S}{\partial z} = \sigma_S (N_2 - N_1)I_S$ <br>     Pump Intensity: $\frac{\partial I_P}{\partial z} = -\sigma_p N_1 I_P$ <br>  <br>     The population densities of the two levels, $N_1$ and $N_2$, are related to the total population density ($N_{tot}$) by the constraint $N_1(z, t) + N_2(z, t) = N_{tot}(z)$. <br>  <br>     In the stationary regime, the population density equation is set to zero ($\frac{\partial N_2}{\partial t} = 0$) and can be written as: <br>  <br>     \[ 0 = \frac{\sigma_p I_p}{h\nu_p}N_1 - \frac{\sigma_S I_S}{h\nu_S}(N_2 - N_1) - \frac{1}{\tau_{Sp}}N_2 \] <br>  <br>     When these expressions for $N_1$ and $N_2$ are substituted back into the intensity equations, the rate equations in the stationary regime can be derived as: <br>  <br>     \[ \frac{dI_p}{dz} = -\sigma_p N_{tot}I_p \frac{1 + \frac{I_S}{I_{S,Sat}}}{1 + \frac{I_p}{I_{p,Sat}} + 2 \frac{I_S}{I_{S,Sat}}} \] <br>  <br>     \[ \frac{dI_S}{dz} = \sigma_S N_{tot}I_S \frac{\frac{I_p}{I_{p,Sat}} - 1}{1 + \frac{I_p}{I_{p,Sat}} + 2 \frac{I_S}{I_{S,Sat}}} \]
What are the main Erbium parameters to check the performance of an EDFA?	The main Erbium parameters related to the performance of an Erbium Doped Fiber Amplifier (EDFA) are: <br>     \begin{itemize} <br>         \item Gain ($G$): This is the ratio of the output signal power ($P_{out}(\nu_s)$) to the input signal power ($P_{in}(\nu_s)$). <br>         \item Power Conversion Efficiency ($PCE$): This is the ratio of the difference between the output and input signal power to the input pump power. The maximum theoretical value for PCE is $\frac{\nu_S}{\nu_P} = \frac{\lambda_P}{\lambda_S}$. <br>         \item Saturation Input Power ($P_{in}^{sat}$): This is the input signal power at which the EDFA gain decreases by 3 dB with respect to its maximum gain ($G_{max}$). <br>         \item Saturation Output Power ($P_{out}^{sat}$): This is the output signal power at which the EDFA gain decreases by 3 dB with respect to its maximum gain. <br>         \item Bandwidth: shape due to $\omega_s$, kind of a mountain shape on a wavelength-over-gain chart. It is the range of frequencies over which the gain is at least $G_{max} - 3 dB$. <br>     \end{itemize}
What is ASE (Amplified Spontaneous Emission)?	Amplified Spontaneous Emission (ASE) is a source of noise in an Erbium Doped Fiber Amplifier (EDFA). <br>  <br>     The equation that describes the average number of photons at signal frequency ($\langle n_S \rangle$), including both the signal and ASE, is: <br>  <br>     \[ \frac{d\langle n_S\rangle}{dz}=\sigma_S(N_2-N_1)\langle n_S\rangle+2\sigma_S N_2 \] <br>  <br>     where: <br>     \begin{itemize} <br>         \item $\sigma_S(N_2-N_1)\langle n_S\rangle$ is the average number of photons generated, per unit length, by stimulated emission at the signal frequency, minus the average number of photons absorbed, per unit length, by stimulated absorption at the signal frequency. <br>         \item $2\sigma_S N_2$ is the forcing term that generates ASE. This term qualitatively corresponds to the average number of photons generated, per unit length, by the emission stimulated by 2 spontaneous photons. The factor of 2 stems from the fact that a single-mode fiber has 2 degenerate modes. <br>     \end{itemize}
What is the noise figure of an EDFA?	The noise figure ($\text{NF}$) of an Erbium Doped Fiber Amplifier (EDFA) is a parameter used to quantify the degradation of the Optical Signal-to-Noise Ratio ($\text{OSNR}$) caused by the amplifier. <br>  <br>     The noise figure is defined as the ratio of the input OSNR to the output OSNR: <br>     \[ \text{NF} = \frac{\text{OSNR}_{\text{in}}}{\text{OSNR}_{\text{out}}} > 1 \text{} \] <br>  <br>     In decibels ($\text{dB}$), the noise figure is expressed as $\text{NF}_{\text{dB}} > 3 \text{ dB}$, because for an EDFA, the noise figure is $\text{NF} > 2$, which represents the minimum possible noise (quantum noise). <br>  <br>     For an amplifier with gain $G \gg 1$, the noise figure can be approximated as: <br>     \[ \text{NF} \approx 2 \cdot n_{\text{sp}} \text{} \] <br>     where $n_{\text{sp}} = \frac{N_2}{N_2 - N_1} \approx 1$ is the spontaneous emission factor. <br>  <br>     Generally, a higher gain amplifier has a lower noise figure. However, when the amplifier operates in the saturation regime, the gain is lower, and the noise figure is higher.
How can EDFA be used for?	\begin{itemize} <br>         \item As a \textbf{line amplifier} along the link or \textbf{pre-amplifier} before the receiver. The low power input signal is strongly amplified with minimal degradation of the OSNR. <br>         \item As a \textbf{booster amplifier} at the transmitter to increase the launch power into the fiber. Both NF and OSNR are high, so there is margin for degradation. <br>     \end{itemize}
