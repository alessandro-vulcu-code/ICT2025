Exercise 1 Let P be a set of points and let S = {c1 , c2 , . . . , ck } ⊂ P be the optimal
solution to k-center with z outliers (the problem tackled by Homeworks 2-3). Call ZS the
set of outliers, that is the set of z points of P farthest from S and, for 1 ≤ i ≤ k, define
Ci as the set of points of P − ZS closest to ci (ties broken arbitrarily). Thus, the optimal
value of the objective function is:

                        Φopt = max d(x, S) = max max d(x, ci ).
                               x∈P −ZS            1≤i≤k   x∈Ci

Given an arbitrary set X of k + z + 1 points of P :
  1. Show that there must exist a Ci that contains at least 2 non-outlier points of X (i.e.,
     at least 2 points of X ∩ (P − ZS )).

  2. Let dmin (X) be the minimum distance between any 2 points of X. Using (a), show
     that dmin (X) ≤ 2Φopt .

  3. Based on the claim of (b), derive a bound on the initial guess rmin used by the
     sequential algorithm for k-center with z outliers implemented in Homework 2.

Solution.
  1. X must contain at least |X| − z = k + 1 points of P − ZS . Since the Ci ’s partition
     P −ZS into k subsets, there must exist a Ci containing at least 2 points of X∩(P −ZS ).

  2. Let p, q be two points of X ∩ (P − ZS ) belonging to the same subset Ci , which must
     exist by virtue of (a). Clearly,

                         dmin (X) ≤ d(p, q) ≤ d(p, ci ) + d(ci , q) ≤ 2Φopt ,

     where inequality d(p, q) ≤ d(p, ci ) + d(ci , q) derives from the triangle inequality.

  3. The sequential algorithm for k-center with z outliers implemented in Homework 2,
     sets the initial guess rmin as dmin (X)/2, where X are the first k + z + 1 points of the
     input dataset P . Since the previous point holds for an arbitrary subset X ⊆ P of
     k + z + 1 points, we have that rmin = dmin (X)/2 ≤ Φopt

Exercise 2 Let T be a dataset of transactions over a set of items I = I1 ∪ I2 , where I1
and I2 are disjoint. An itemset X is said to be fair if it has even length and its items are
half from I1 and half from I2 . For a given support threshold minsup, let F2k be the set of
frequent fair itemsets of length 2k. Two itemsets X, Y ∈ F2k are compatible if they have
in common exactly k − 1 items of I1 and k − 1 items of I2 . For k ≥ 2, define

            C2k+2 = {X ∪ Y : X, Y ∈ F2k , such that X and Y are compatible}

Show that F2k+2 ⊆ C2k+2 .

Solution. Let Z = Z[1]Z[2] · · · Z[2k + 2] be an arbitrary itemset of F2k+2 and, w.l.o.g.,
assume that Z[i] ∈ I1 , for 1 ≤ i ≤ k + 1, and Z[i] ∈ I2 , for k + 2 ≤ i ≤ 2k + 2. Define the
following two itemsets

                             X = Z − {Z[k + 1], Z[2k + 2]}
                             Y = Z − {Z[k], Z[2k + 1]}


Note that Z = X ∪ Y and |X| = |Y | = 2k. By definition, both X and Y are subsets of Z
and contain k items of I1 and k items of I2 (i.e., they are fair), hence X, Y ∈ F2k . Moreover,
X and Y are compatible since they share exactly k − 1 items of I1 (Z[1]Z[2] · · · Z[k − 1])
and k − 1 items of I2 (Z[k + 2]Z[k + 3] · · · Z[2k]). Therefore, Z = X ∪ Y must be included
in C2k+2 .                                                                                   
