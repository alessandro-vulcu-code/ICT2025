## The Human Visual System

### The Eye

Two types of retinal receptors:

| Receptor | Count | Function | Conditions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cones** | 6–7 million (center) | Color, high resolution | Requires high illumination |
| **Rods** | 75–150 million | Light intensity, low resolution | Very sensitive in low light |
![[Pasted image 20260501163251.png]]
### Light Perception

- Perceived intensity: **logarithmic function** of physical intensity
- Physical dynamic range: $\approx 10^{10}$ (100 dB)
- HVS cannot operate over the full range simultaneously → adapts via **global lighting changes** (reduced dynamic range at any given moment)
- Perceived brightness is not a simple function of luminance — context matters
![[Pasted image 20260522103557.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260522103612.png]]


### Contrast Sensitivity Function (CSF)

> [!Important] Contrast Sensitivity Function (CSF)
> The CSF measures HVS ability to detect luminance differences across **spatial frequencies**.
>
> - **Contrast**: difference in luminance between lightest and darkest parts of a pattern (%)
> - **Spatial frequency**: measured in **cycles per degree** of visual angle
> - **Sensitivity** = $1 / \text{(minimum detectable contrast)}$
>
> CSF **peaks at mid-range frequencies** (≈ 2–5 cycles/degree) and declines at both low and high frequencies.
>
> **Implication for compression:** high-frequency errors are less visible → can apply coarser quantization to high frequencies.
![[Pasted image 20260501163356.png]]
### Color Perception

- Visible light: **400–700 nm**
- Cone distribution by color sensitivity:
  - 65% sensitive to **red** (~575 nm)
  - 33% sensitive to **green** (~535 nm)
  - 2% sensitive to **blue** (~445 nm) — but highly sensitive
![[Pasted image 20260501163417.png]]

- **Tristimulus**: color sensation corresponds to the 3D response vector of the three cone types
- Any color = weighted combination of three primary colors

### Color Spaces

**RGB** — unit cube representation:
- $(0,0,0)$: Black; $(1,1,1)$: White; main diagonal: Grayscale
- Primaries at $(1,0,0)$, $(0,1,0)$, $(0,0,1)$
- Secondaries: Cyan $(0,1,1)$, Magenta $(1,0,1)$, Yellow $(1,1,0)$
![[Pasted image 20260501163800.png]]
**HSV** — derived by balancing RGB cube on black corner, rotating so black-white diagonal is vertical, projecting:
- **Hue** (H): angle 0°–360° around vertical axis → color family
- **Saturation** (S): distance from central axis → color purity
- **Value** (V): vertical position 0–1 → brightness

**YCbCr** (≈ digital YUV):
- **Y**: luminance (same as HSV Value)
- **Cb, Cr**: chrominance (Cartesian representation vs. polar S/H in HSV)
- $Cb \approx B - Y$, $Cr \approx R - Y$

### RGB to YCbCr Conversion

> [!Important] ITU-R BT.601 Color Conversion
> $$\begin{bmatrix} Y \\ Cb \\ Cr \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.299 & 0.587 & 0.114 \\ -0.1687 & -0.3313 & 0.5 \\ 0.5 & -0.4187 & -0.0813 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R \\ G \\ B \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 128 \\ 128 \end{bmatrix}$$
>
> - **Y weights** reflect HVS sensitivity: green weighted most (0.587), blue least (0.114)
> - **Cb, Cr offset** (+128) centers the 8-bit range $[0,255]$
> - **Invertible**: $\mathbf{p}_{RGB} = M^{-1}(\mathbf{p}_{YCbCr} - \mathbf{o})$ — perfect reconstruction

### Beyond HVS: Machine-Centric Multimedia

A growing fraction of multimedia content is consumed by **algorithms** (surveillance, IoT, industrial monitoring), not humans.

| Aspect | Human Visual System | Machine Models |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Priority | Semantic regions (faces), CSF-limited | High-frequency details, local textures |
| Goal | Minimize perceived distortion | Maximize downstream task accuracy |
| Compression strategy | Psychovisually lossless | Discard "semantically irrelevant" content |

**Task-Oriented Communications**: if the end-user is a machine, extreme compression ratios are possible by discarding visually important but semantically irrelevant information.

## Sound Perception

### Pure Tone Perception

A pure tone $x(t) = a\sin(2\pi f_1 t)$ with power $\sigma^2 = a^2/2$ excites **multiple nerve fibers** simultaneously (power spreading).

**Filterbank model**: $M$ filters, each corresponding to one nerve fiber $k$:
- Frequency response: $H_k(f) = A_k(f)\exp^{j\phi_k(f)}$
- Response to tone at $f_1$: $y_k(t) = a A_k(f_1)\sin[2\pi f_1 t + \phi_k(f_1)]$
- **Spreading function**: $S_E(k) = A_k^2(f_1)$ — power distribution across fibers

### Hearing Threshold
![[Pasted image 20260501163839.png]]
- **Absolute hearing threshold** $S_a(f)$: minimum power for a tone at frequency $f$ to be audible
- Minimum of $S_a(f)$ at 1–4 kHz → HVS most sensitive in speech frequency range
- Compression implication: components below $S_a(f)$ can be discarded

### Critical Band

> [!Important] Critical Band (CB)
> For $N$ sinusoids *close* to frequency $f_1$, the audibility condition is:
> $$\sum_i \sigma_i^2 > S_a(f_1)$$
> (energy integrates within the critical band)
>
> Sinusoids are "near" if they fall within the **Critical Band** centered at $f_1$. CB width is a function of $f_1$.
![[Pasted image 20260501163938.png|500]]

### Masking Curves

#### Frequency Masking

**Definition:** $S_m(f_0, \sigma^2, f)$ = minimum power of a tone at $f$ to be audible when a masker at $f_0$ with power $\sigma^2 > S_a(f_0)$ is playing.
![[Pasted image 20260501164014.png]]

**Properties of $S_m(f_0, \sigma^2, f)$**:
- Triangular shape around $f_0$; maximum at $f = f_0$
- **Masking index**: $S_m(f, \sigma^2, f) - \sigma^2 < 0$ — masked tone need not be louder than masker
- Asymmetric: slower decrease toward higher frequencies
- Slope proportional to CB width
- Slope (toward higher $f$) decreases as masker power $\sigma^2$ increases
![[Pasted image 20260522103757.png]]
#### Time Masking
![[Pasted image 20260501164647.png]]
- **Pre-masking**: 2–5 ms before onset of masker
- **Post-masking**: 100–200 ms after masker ends

### Model Application

Psychoacoustical model enables:
- Identify **non-audible signal components**
- Allow quantization noise up to the masking threshold

**Limitations**:
- Model valid only for pure tones or narrowband sounds
- Only 3-way simultaneous interactions modeled
- Real signals are far more complex → parameters determined **experimentally** through large-scale listening tests

## Image and Video Representation

### Gray Level Images

- Discrete $N \times M$ grid
- Raster-order scan: position $(m,n)$ → index $k = (n-1)M + m$
- $f_{n,m} = f_k$
![[Pasted image 20260501164756.png]]
### Color Representations

**RGB**: three gray-scale components $R, G, B$ — one image per channel.
![[Pasted image 20260501164937.png]]
**YUV/YCbCr**: one luminance (Y) + two chrominance (U/Cb, V/Cr) components.
![[Pasted image 20260501164947.png]]
### Chroma Subsampling

Sampling scheme notation: $J : a : b$

- $J$: reference horizontal size (usually 4)
- $a$: chroma samples on first line of reference pattern
- $b$: chroma samples on second line
![[Pasted image 20260501165005.png]]

| Scheme | Horiz. Chroma Res. | Vert. Chroma Res. |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **4:4:4** | Full | Full |
| **4:2:2** | 1/2 | Full |
| **4:2:0** | 1/2 | 1/2 |
| **4:1:1** | 1/4 | Full |

### YCbCr 4:2:0

Most common format in video compression (JPEG, H.264, HEVC, AV1).

- **Y**: full resolution
- **Cb, Cr**: half resolution horizontally and vertically

**Compression ratio** vs full RGB:
$$\frac{4+1+1}{4+4+4} = \frac{6}{12} = 0.5 \quad \Rightarrow \quad 50\% \text{ data reduction}$$

**Perceptual basis**: HVS is far more sensitive to luminance (brightness) variations than to chrominance (color) variations → reduced Cb/Cr resolution is largely imperceptible.

### Quantization

- Samples represented on a discrete set: **uniform quantization** (rounding)
- $L$ = number of levels; $b = \log_2 L$ bits per sample
- Standard: $b = 8$ bits per component
  - 256 gray levels (8 bpp)
  - $256^3 \approx 16\text{M}$ colors (24 bpp for RGB)
- High Dynamic Range (HDR): 32–64 bits per channel

### Digital Video Representation

$$I : (n, m, T, c) \rightarrow x \in \{0, 1, \ldots, 2^b - 1\}$$

- $(n,m)$: spatial position
- $T$: time index (frame)
- $c$: color component
- Sequence of digital images with temporal dependencies, 3 color components (RGB or YUV), possible chroma subsampling

## Compression Principles

### Motivation

> [!Example] HD DVB Uncompressed Data Rate
> **System:** 1 luminance component $1920\times1080$ + 2 chrominance components $960\times540$, 8-bit quantization, 50 fps
>
> $$R = (1920\times1080 + 2\times960\times540)\times8\times50 \approx 1.24 \text{ Gbps}$$
>
> **2-hour movie storage:** $\approx 1.12$ TB
>
> **Conclusion:** compression is essential.

### Compression Fundamentals

Three sources of redundancy:

1. **Statistical redundancy**
   - *Spatial*: images are locally homogeneous (neighboring pixels are correlated)
   - *Temporal*: successive video frames are highly correlated

2. **Psychovisual redundancy**
   - Limits of HVS: CSF, masking, contour sensitivity
   - Goal: **"Visually lossless"** coding — introduce errors below perception threshold

3. **Semantic redundancy** *(emerging)*
   - Images contain recognizable structures (objects, concepts)
   - Goal: **"Generative" coding** — transmit tokens/meaning rather than pixels

*Modern algorithms should exploit all three.*

### Lossless and Lossy Algorithms

| Property | Lossless | Lossy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Reconstruction | Perfect | Approximate |
| Basis | Statistical redundancy only | Quantization + psychovisual redundancy |
| Compression ratio | Small ($T \leq 3$) | High ($T \approx 5$ to $\gg$) |

### Basic Tools for Compression
![[Pasted image 20260501165037.png]]
1. **Transform**: Linear (DCT, Wavelet) or Non-linear (Neural) — concentrate energy into few coefficients
2. **Prediction**: Spatial or temporal redundancy reduction
3. **Quantization**: Rate reduction through approximation (**the only lossy step**)
4. **Entropy Coding**: Residual redundancy reduction (VLC, Arithmetic coding)

### Rate and Compression Ratio

**Compression ratio**:
$$T = \frac{B_{\text{in}}}{B_{\text{out}}} = \frac{R_{\text{in}}}{R_{\text{out}}}$$

**Coding rate**:
- Image: $R = B_{\text{out}} / (NM)$ [bpp — bits per pixel]
- Video/audio: $R = B_{\text{out}} / T$ [bps]

| Application | Typical $T$ |
| :--- | :--- |
| Lossless image coding | $T \leq 3$ |
| Lossy image coding | $T \approx 5 \to ?$ |
| Lossy video coding | $T \approx 20 \to ?$ |

### Quality and Distortion Metrics

#### Non-Perceptual Objective Criteria (NP-OC)

**Error image**: $\mathcal{E}(f, \tilde{f}) = f - \tilde{f}$

> [!Important] Mean Square Error (MSE) and PSNR
> $$\mathcal{D}(f,\tilde{f}) = \frac{1}{NM}\|\mathcal{E}\|^2 = \frac{1}{NM}\sum_{n,m}\mathcal{E}_{n,m}^2$$
>
> $$\text{PSNR}(f,\tilde{f}) = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{255^2}{\mathcal{D}(f,\tilde{f})}\right) \quad [\text{dB}]$$
>
> **Pros:** simple, analytically derivable, linked to $\mathcal{L}^2$ norm.
> **Cons:** does not model HVS — same MSE can yield very different perceived quality.

#### Perceptual Objective Criteria

**Weighted PSNR (WPSNR)**: apply a frequency weighting filter $h$ (modeling CSF):
$$\text{WPSNR}(f,\tilde{f}) = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{255^2}{\mathcal{D}_W}\right), \qquad \mathcal{D}_W = \frac{1}{NM}\|h * \mathcal{E}\|^2$$
![[Pasted image 20260501165141.png]]
> [!Important] Structural Similarity Index (SSIM)
> For image blocks $x$ and $y$ (with $\alpha=\beta=\gamma=1$, $C_3=C_2/2$):
> $$\text{SSIM}(x,y) = \frac{(2\mu_x\mu_y + C_1)(2\sigma_{xy} + C_2)}{(\mu_x^2+\mu_y^2+C_1)(\sigma_x^2+\sigma_y^2+C_2)}$$
>
> Three components:
> - **Luminance**: $l(x,y) = \frac{2\mu_x\mu_y + C_1}{\mu_x^2+\mu_y^2+C_1}$
> - **Contrast**: $c(x,y) = \frac{2\sigma_x\sigma_y + C_2}{\sigma_x^2+\sigma_y^2+C_2}$
> - **Structure**: $s(x,y) = \frac{\sigma_{xy}+C_3}{\sigma_x\sigma_y+C_3}$
>
> Image SSIM = average over all blocks. Range $[0,1]$, 1 = identical.

**NN-based metrics (LPIPS)**:
- PSNR/SSIM fail to capture "realism" in highly compressed or AI-generated content
- **LPIPS** (*Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity*): uses pre-trained NNs (VGG, ViT) as feature extractors; measures distance in feature space rather than pixel space
- Key insight: a signal can have low PSNR but high perceptual plausibility

#### Subjective Criteria (SC)

- Ground truth for quality evaluation: human observer experiments
- Expensive, slow, statistically demanding
- NP-OC often used as proxy (simplicity, geometric interpretation, analytical optimization) — but correlation with SC is imperfect

*Pepper images with different error types (same MSE=16): distributed noise, concentrated block, contour-based, high-frequency, chroma subsampling*

*All images have the same MSE = 16, yet perceived quality varies enormously. SSIM captures this better than MSE.*

| Error Type | MSE | SSIM |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Distributed white noise ($\sigma=4$) | 16 | 0.906 |
| Concentrated over $100\times100$ pixels | 16 | 0.972 |
| Concentrated on contours (Sobel filter) | 16 | 0.987 |
| Noise on high spatial frequencies | 16 | 0.882 |
| Chroma subsampling | 21.27 | — |

**Key observation**: the same MSE produces very different perceptual quality. Contour errors are least visible (SSIM 0.987); high-frequency noise most visible (SSIM 0.882).

### Video Perception

- HVS has both spatial and **temporal** sensitivity
- **Spatio-temporal CSF**: sensitivity depends on both spatial frequency and temporal frequency
- Spatial and temporal **masking** effects apply
- **Scene changes** cause sudden sensitivity drops (masking → can tolerate more distortion just after a cut)
![[Pasted image 20260501165357.png]]
### Complexity, Delay and Robustness

Three additional performance axes (beyond rate/quality):

- **Complexity**: limited by real-time constraints, hardware, economic cost
- **Delay**: measured at encoder; related to complexity but mainly driven by **coding order** (how far ahead encoder must look)
- **Robustness**: sensitivity of compressed stream to transmission losses

**Design tensions**:

| Improve | Conflicts with |
| :--- | :--- |
| ↑ Quality | ↓ Rate |
| ↑ Robustness | ↓ Complexity |
| — | ↓ Delay |

Modern system design must balance all five: quality, rate, complexity, delay, robustness.

**Modern quality assessment approach**: report all three:
- **PSNR** (simple, pixel-based)
- **SSIM** (structure-based)
- **LPIPS** (feature-based, perceptual)
