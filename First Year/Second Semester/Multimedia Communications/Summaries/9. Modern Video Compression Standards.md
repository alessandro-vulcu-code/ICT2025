## The Hybrid Video Coding Paradigm

### The Universal Hybrid Video Encoder

From MPEG-1 to VVC, every standard uses the same fundamental **hybrid video coding** architecture.

**Core processes:**

- **Spatial prediction**, also called *Intra prediction*.
- **Temporal prediction**, also called *Inter prediction*.
- **Residual calculation**, obtained as the difference between the current signal and the prediction.
- **Transform and quantization**, which decorrelate and compress the residual.
- An **internal closed loop**, using inverse quantization and inverse transform, to reconstruct the same reference frames available at the decoder and prevent *drift*.

> [!Important] Universal Hybrid Encoder
> **Statement:** Modern standards from MPEG-1 to VVC are based on spatial and temporal prediction, residual coding, transform, quantization, entropy coding, and an internal reconstruction loop.
>
> **Interpretation:** The encoder must reconstruct its own decoded frames because future motion compensation uses decoded references, not original frames. This keeps the encoder and decoder synchronized and prevents drift.
![[Pasted image 20260506125516.png]]
*Block diagram of the universal hybrid video encoder. The input frame $\hat{A}$ enters a summation node that produces residual $e_B$. The residual passes through two stages of $4 \times 4$ DCT, quantization, and CABAC/CAVLC to form the output bitstream. The reconstruction loop contains inverse quantization, inverse DCT, reconstructed residual $\hat{e}_B$, summation with prediction, frame buffer, deblocking filter, and reconstructed frames $\hat{f}_p$ / $\hat{f}_b$. A mode decision block selects between Intra Prediction and Motion Compensation, while Motion Estimation feeds Motion Compensation from the Frame Buffer.*
### Scope of the Standard

If the fundamental architecture is universal, the key question is: what exactly is standardized?

![[Pasted image 20260506125634.png]]

> [!Important] Interoperability and Competition
> **Statement:** Standards exclusively define the bitstream syntax and the decoding process. Encoder architecture and optimization strategies remain open problems for industrial competition.
>
> **Interpretation:** Any compliant decoder must decode a valid bitstream in the same way. Encoders, however, can compete through better mode decisions, faster search strategies, rate control, perceptual optimization, and hardware implementation.

### Codecs Historical Evolution

To improve compression, standards continuously increase the encoder's **degrees of freedom**, especially partitioning options and prediction modes.

![[Pasted image 20260506125655.png]]

> [!Important] The Golden Rule
> **Statement:** Generational leaps typically halve the required bit-rate for identical perceptual quality, with an encoder computational complexity increase of approximately $10\times$.
>
> **Interpretation:** Each new codec generation improves compression efficiency, but the encoder must search a much larger space of partitions, prediction modes, transforms, filters, and signaling choices.

### The Cost of Freedom

The empirical data shows that newer standards achieve significantly better PSNR at the same bit-rate, but require ever increasing encoding time.

- **MPEG-2 underflow:** The encoder reaches its maximum quantization limit. It lacks the tools to discard further bits, causing severe target-rate overshoots of 7-8 Mbps.
- **The RDO challenge:** VVC and AV1 have a massive tree of possible block partitions and prediction modes. Finding the optimal configuration under 2.0 Mbps while maximizing quality requires evaluating millions of combinations.

**How does the encoder make these decisions?**

$$
\rightarrow \text{Rate-Distortion Optimization}
$$

## Space Partitioning and RDO

### Recall: Rate-Distortion Optimization

From the previous lecture, the encoder minimizes the Lagrangian cost function for each block:

$$
J = D + \lambda R \tag{1}
$$

> [!Important] Rate-Distortion Optimization
> **Statement:** For each coding choice, the encoder minimizes
>
> $$
> J = D + \lambda R
> $$
>
> where $D$ is distortion, $R$ is rate, and $\lambda$ controls the tradeoff between quality and bitrate.
>
> **Interpretation:** A mode is not selected only because it has low distortion or low rate. It is selected because it minimizes their weighted combination.

**The block partitioning problem:**

- Partitioning a block into sub-blocks is treated as a distinct coding mode.
- The encoder evaluates the cost of the original block, $J_B$, against the sum of the sub-block costs:

$$
J_{split} = \sum J_{subblock}
$$

- If $J_{split} < J_B$, the split is accepted.
- *The evolution of video standards is largely the evolution of allowable split geometries.*

### H.264/AVC: The Macroblock Era

H.264 relies on a fixed and rigid structure, which limits the RDO search space.

- **Base unit:** the $16 \times 16$ **Macroblock** (MB).
- **Variable Block-Size (VBS):** the MB can be partitioned into $16 \times 8$, $8 \times 16$, or $8 \times 8$ blocks to better capture motion boundaries.
- **Sub-partitions:** $8 \times 8$ blocks can be further divided down to $4 \times 4$.

> [!Important] Limitation of the Macroblock
> **Statement:** A maximum block size of $16 \times 16$ is highly inefficient for High Definition (1080p) or 4K video, where smooth background areas such as sky or walls can span hundreds of pixels.
>
> **Interpretation:** Large smooth regions should be represented by large blocks. If the standard only permits small blocks, the encoder must spend unnecessary bits on partition signaling and local prediction.

### H.265/HEVC: The Quad-Tree Paradigm

HEVC abandons macroblocks in favor of the **Coding Tree Unit (CTU)** to handle UHD resolutions.

- **Larger base size:** CTUs can be up to $64 \times 64$ pixels.
- **Quad-Tree split:** a CTU is recursively split into four square **Coding Units** (CUs), down to $4 \times 4$ CUs.
- **Functional separation:** a CU can be symmetrically or asymmetrically divided into **Prediction Units** (PUs) and **Transform Units** (TUs).

![[Pasted image 20260506125913.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260506125928.png]]
### H.266/VVC: QT + Multi-Type Tree (MTT)

VVC introduces extreme flexibility and eliminates the strict separation between CUs, PUs, and TUs for most modes.

- **Massive base size:** CTUs can be up to $128 \times 128$ pixels.
- **QT + MTT:** Quad-Tree leaves can be further partitioned using a **Multi-Type Tree**.
- **New splits:** includes *Binary* splits, which are symmetric halves, and *Ternary* splits, with a $1:2:1$ ratio.
- Rectangular blocks become fundamental for encoding object edges accurately.
![[Pasted image 20260506125946.png]]
### AV1: 10-Way Superblock Partitioning

AV1 uses a similar highly flexible hierarchical approach.

- **Base unit:** the *Superblock*, sized up to $128 \times 128$.
- **10-way split:** a block can be partitioned using 10 different geometrical patterns.
- The available geometries include $4:1$ and $1:4$ rectangular splits, creating highly elongated blocks for sharp directional textures.
- Square blocks can be recursively partitioned.

![[Pasted image 20260506130003.png]]

### The Algorithmic Burden of RDO

> [!Important] Decision Tree Search
> **Statement:** To find the optimal partition, the encoder explores the block geometry using a **Depth-First Search (DFS)** algorithm while optimizing the cost
>
> $$
> J = D + \lambda R.
> $$
>
> **Interpretation:** More partition freedom improves compression only if the encoder can afford to search the corresponding decision tree.

Idea: use the encoded block to predict the next block -> sequential
The evaluation is a massive sequential process:

1. **Top-down exploration:** try coding the block as a whole, then try splitting it.
2. **Causal dependency:** sub-blocks are tested sequentially in Z-scan order. A block cannot be predicted until its top and left neighboring blocks have been fully *reconstructed*, so their pixels are frozen.
3. **Inner loop for prediction:** for each block, exhaustively test Intra and Inter modes to find the local minimum $J_{CU}$.
4. **Bottom-up decision:** compare the total cost. If

$$
\sum J_{children} < J_{parent},
$$

the split is kept.

**Conclusion:** The theoretical state space of a CTU is enormous, but reference models traverse it sequentially. Commercial encoders drastically reduce this process using **fast heuristics** and **machine learning** to prune the tree early.

### Pruning the Decision Tree: Fast Heuristics

To mitigate the extreme complexity of RDO, production encoders such as VVenC and SVT-AV1 implement aggressive early termination strategies.

**Common pruning approaches:**

- **Spatial/texture analysis:** evaluate local variance and directional gradients. If a $64 \times 64$ Coding Unit has low variance, as in a flat background, deeper QT/MTT partitions are immediately bypassed.
- **Temporal correlation:** constrain the RDO search space by exploiting the partition depth of the co-located CU in the reference frame.
- **Machine learning classifiers:** use lightweight CNNs or gradient boosting decision trees, such as LightGBM, to predict split probabilities directly from raw pixel features and discard highly improbable geometries before any RDO calculation.

## Spatial and Temporal Prediction

### Intra Prediction

**Intra prediction** exploits spatial redundancy by extrapolating pixels from already-decoded neighboring blocks.
![[Pasted image 20260506130740.png|500]]

**The evolution of freedom:**

- **H.264/AVC:** 4 prediction types for $16 \times 16$ blocks, and 9 types for $4 \times 4$ blocks: 8 directions plus DC.
- **H.265/HEVC:** expands to 35 modes: 33 directions plus DC and Planar, to handle complex textures.
- **H.266/VVC:** expands to 65 directional modes, plus wide-angle modes for non-square blocks.

### The Cost of Freedom: Predicting the Predictor

> [!Important] Overhead Problem
> **Statement:** If VVC has 67 Intra modes, sending the mode index requires approximately 7 bits per block. For tiny $4 \times 4$ blocks, this signaling overhead would completely destroy the compression gains.
>
> **Interpretation:** Increasing the number of modes improves prediction quality, but the selected mode must also be transmitted. The mode index itself can become too expensive.

**Solution: Most Probable Mode (MPM)**

- The encoder predicts the mode itself using the spatial context.
- A list of **Most Probable Modes** (MPM) is built using the directions chosen by the top and left neighbors.
- HEVC uses a 3-candidate MPM list.
- VVC expands this to a 6-candidate MPM list.
- If the chosen mode is in the MPM list, it is coded with a very short index, for example 1 or 2 bits.

### Inter Prediction: Fractional Motion Compensation

**Inter prediction** removes temporal redundancy through **Motion Estimation** and **Motion Compensation** (ME/MC).

- Objects rarely move by exact integer pixel amounts between frames.
- **H.264/HEVC/VVC:** use quarter-pel, or $1/4$-pixel, fractional precision. The encoder generates sub-pixels using interpolation filters, such as the 6-tap filters in H.264.
- **AV1:** pushes precision further to $1/8$-pixel accuracy and uses dynamically selectable interpolation filters, such as sharp and smooth filters.

### Advanced Motion Compensation

Modern standards generalize temporal distance. A frame is no longer restricted to predicting from its immediate chronological neighbor.

- **Multiple reference frames:** slices can maintain one P-list or two B-lists of reference images kept in the **Decoded Picture Buffer** (DPB).
- **Generalized B-slices:** the encoder can flexibly select predictors from these lists and combine them using weighted averages.

![[Pasted image 20260506133134.png]]

### Vector Prediction: From Skip to Merge

Just like Intra modes, **Motion Vectors** (MVs) must be predicted to save bits.

- **H.264 Skip/Direct:** uses a simple median of spatial neighbors. If the median is correct, 0 bits are spent on MVs.
- **HEVC/VVC Merge Mode:** replaces the median with a dynamic *candidate list*.
- The list aggregates 5 spatial candidates from adjacent blocks and 1 temporal candidate from the co-located block in the reference frame.
- The encoder transmits only the index of the best candidate in the list, sharing both the MV and the reference frame index.

### Beyond Translation: Modern Inter Tools

To squeeze the final bits of temporal redundancy out of the signal, AV1 and VVC abandon the assumption that blocks only move rigidly in 2D space.

**Affine Motion Prediction:**

- Allows blocks to rotate, zoom, and shear.
- Uses 4-parameter or 6-parameter models instead of a simple $(x, y)$ vector.

**Compound Prediction:**

- AV1 allows Inter and Intra predictions to be combined within the same block.

## Transforms, Quantization, and Filters

### Residual Coding: The Evolution of Transforms

After prediction, the residual error must be decorrelated into the frequency domain.

**The push for larger and flexible transforms:**

- **H.264/AVC:** relies on a $4 \times 4$ integer-coefficient approximation of the DCT, perfectly synchronized to avoid precision errors between encoder and decoder.
- **H.265/HEVC:** expands transforms up to $32 \times 32$ to handle 4K resolutions. It also introduces a DST, or *Discrete Sine Transform*, approximation specifically for $4 \times 4$ Intra blocks, improving compression by approximately $1\%$.
- **H.266/VVC:** introduces **Multiple Transform Selection** (MTS) and non-square transforms to match the rectangular partitions created by QT+MTT.

### Quantization and Entropy Coding

**Quantization** is the only lossy step in the hybrid encoder.

**Quantization Parameter (QP):**

- In H.264/HEVC, the quantization step size doubles for every increment of 6 in the **Quantization Parameter** (QP).
- Empirically, a unitary QP increment increases the rate by approximately $12.5\%$.

> [!Important] Quantization Parameter Behavior
> **Statement:** In H.264/HEVC, the quantization step size doubles whenever QP increases by 6. Empirically, increasing QP by 1 changes the rate by approximately $12.5\%$.
>
> **Interpretation:** QP is the main knob for rate control. Higher QP means coarser quantization, lower bitrate, and higher distortion.

**Lossless entropy coding:**

- Modern standards are standardized around **CABAC**, or *Context-Adaptive Binary Arithmetic Coding*.
- CABAC transforms syntax elements into binary formats and dynamically adapts probabilities based on previously decoded spatial contexts.
- CABAC yields a 5%-15% rate reduction over older variable-length codes such as CAVLC.

### The Need for In-Loop Filtering

Because transform and quantization are applied independently to disjoint blocks, strong discontinuities, called **blocking artifacts**, appear at block boundaries at low bit-rates.

![[Pasted image 20260506134648.png]]

If left uncorrected, these artifacts are saved in the Decoded Picture Buffer (DPB) and permanently damage future motion compensations. But here we know where we have those artifacts: around the contours. 

### Evolution of In-Loop Filters

To fix quantization damage, standards mandate complex **normative filters** inside the reconstruction loop.

- **H.264/AVC Deblocking:** analyzes edges between $4 \times 4$ blocks. Filtering strength adapts dynamically to the coding mode, motion vectors, and quantization step of neighboring blocks.
- **H.265/HEVC Deblocking + SAO:** applies deblocking only on an $8 \times 8$ grid to reduce complexity. It introduces **Sample Adaptive Offset** (SAO), which categorizes pixels by edges and bands, then adds offsets to correct ringing artifacts.

### VVC and AV1 Filters

As transform sizes grew and bit-rates decreased, deblocking and SAO were no longer sufficient. Modern codecs dedicate major computational effort to pixel restoration.

- **H.266/VVC:** adds the **Adaptive Loop Filter** (ALF) and **Luma Mapping with Chroma Scaling** (LMCS), specifically designed to preserve details in HDR, or *High Dynamic Range*, video.
- **AV1:** implements a strong post-processing pipeline:
  - **Directional Filters (CDEF):** analyze gradient directions to smooth edges without blurring textures.
  - **Loop Restoration Filters:** Wiener filters applied over large windows.
  - **Film Grain Synthesis:** denoises the image before encoding, then synthesizes artificial grain at the decoder to recreate the cinematic look at zero bitrate cost.

## Network Abstraction and Parallelism

### Transmission and Network Errors

> [!Important] Fragility of Compression
> **Statement:** A 4K video compressed into a perfect 2 Mbps mathematical stream can be severely corrupted if only a few bits are flipped.
>
> **Interpretation:** Entropy-coded streams are compact but fragile. A bit error can desynchronize CABAC contexts and then propagate through spatial prediction and motion compensation.

**Different "pipes", same vulnerability:**

- **DVB-T broadcast:** a continuous stream subject to electromagnetic noise.
- **Internet, using RTP/UDP or TCP/DASH:** a stream cut into packets with MTU around 1500 bytes; packets can arrive late or be lost.
- **Disk storage, such as MP4:** requires specific indexes to seek.

**Consequence:** CABAC loses context. Errors propagate spatially and temporally through Motion Compensation, creating a "green and gray mosaic".

### Pictures and Slices

To limit error propagation, pictures can be divided into **slices**.

At each new slice, Intra prediction is stopped and CABAC is reset, so parallel encoding and decoding of slices becomes possible.

![[Pasted image 20260506135622.png]]

### The Solution: Separation of Concerns

Starting from H.264, video standards separate **compression** from **transport**.

**The factory analogy:**

![[Pasted image 20260506135653.png]]

- **Video Coding Layer (VCL):** the production department. It handles pure compression: transforms, prediction, and RDO. It generates the *slices* of each compressed picture.
- **Network Abstraction Layer (NAL):** the shipping department. It takes VCL slices, encapsulates them, and adds metadata.
- **Goal:** provide a standard interface to map video over RTP/IP for real-time communication, ISO MP4 for storage, or MPEG-2 TS for diffusion.

### NAL Units

The VCL encodes each picture into one or more slices. The NAL encapsulates the slices into **VCL NALUs**, one per slice, and generates **Non-VCL NALUs**, such as SPS, PPS, and SEI.

- **VCL NALU:** contains modes, MVs, and quantized coefficients of a slice. It can be **Instantaneous Decoding Refresh**, meaning Intra with CABAC reset, or non-IDR.
- **SPS:** **Sequence Parameter Set**, a non-VCL NALU containing important encoding parameters such as picture size and color depth.
- **PPS:** **Picture Parameter Set**, containing per-picture coding options specific to each codec.
- **SEI:** **Supplemental Enhancement Information**, not necessary for decoding but useful for specific tasks such as subtitles, HDR information, user data, and similar metadata.
- **AUD:** **Access Unit Delimiter**, an optional NALU that signals a new picture.

### NALUs and Access Units

> [!Important] Access Unit
> **Statement:** An **Access Unit** (AU) is a logical grouping of all NALUs required to decode a single frame. The decoder identifies its boundaries implicitly.
>
> **Interpretation:** The AU is the frame-level unit of decoding, while NALUs are the transport and syntax units that compose it.

How is this sequence of NALUs delivered?

- If the transport medium is a continuous "blind" tube, such as MPEG-TS or raw `.264` files, use the **Byte-Stream format**.
- If the transport layer already provides boundaries, such as an MP4 container, use the **Packetized format**.

![[Pasted image 20260506140326.png]]

### Format 1: Annex-B Byte-Stream

**Mechanism:** a Start Code, for example `0x00000001`, is injected before every NALU.

**Emulation prevention:** fake bytes are injected into the payload to prevent valid video data from accidentally mimicking a Start Code.

**Pro:** Annex-B is well suited to continuous streaming or broadcasting, where packet boundaries do not exist natively.

**Con:** it introduces CPU overhead for emulation prevention and re-synchronization. The decoder must scan every byte sequentially to find the next Start Code.

![[Pasted image 20260506140340.png]]

### Format 2: Packetized Length-Prefixed

**Mechanism:** a **Length Field**, usually 4 bytes, is written exactly before the NALU header.

**No Start Codes:** emulation prevention is not strictly required for boundary detection.

**Pro:** this format is highly software-friendly. The parser reads the length and performs an $O(1)$ memory jump directly to the next NALU.

**Con:** it is useless if the stream is corrupted or the external container is damaged, because synchronization is lost.

![[Pasted image 20260506140352.png]]

### The Evolution: HEVC, VVC, and AV1

**H.265/HEVC and H.266/VVC:**

- Maintain the Annex-B / Packetized dualism.
- Expand the NALU header from 1 byte to **2 bytes** to include *Temporal ID* and *Layer ID* for spatial and temporal scalability.

**AV1: breaking the paradigm**

- AV1 abandons the MPEG legacy. NALUs are called **OBUs**, or *Open Bitstream Units*.
- **No Start Codes:** AV1 completely drops Annex-B and emulation prevention to save CPU cycles.
- AV1 exclusively uses a length-prefixed approach, even for raw bitstreams.

### Network Friendliness in Action

The NAL enables intelligent network operations without decoding. The NAL header and structure allow intermediate nodes, called **Media Aware Network Elements** (MANEs), to understand and manage the video stream.

**1. Router inspection (MANE):**

- MANEs can read the 1-byte or 2-byte header; this is a form of *Deep Packet Inspection*.
- **Smart dropping:** if the network is congested, the router can selectively drop Non-Reference B-frames without touching critical IDR frames or parameter sets.
- Protection, such as CRC, can be added to sensitive data such as SPS.

**2. RTP transport and fragmentation:**

- If a VCL NALU, such as an entire I-frame slice, exceeds the network MTU of 1500 bytes, it cannot be transmitted as-is.
- The RTP layer slices large NALUs into **Fragmentation Units** (FUs) and reassembles them at the receiver, keeping the video decoder unaware of the network constraints.

### Resynchronization: Stream Structure

![[Pasted image 20260506140635.png]]

Decoding may restart at a **Random Access Point** (RAP).

**IDR (Instantaneous Decoding Refresh):**

An IDR is a strict barrier that completely clears the Decoded Picture Buffer (DPB). No pictures following the IDR in decoding order can reference pictures preceding it.

- **Pros:** guarantees a clean restart. It is ideal for the absolute beginning of a video sequence or hard scene cuts.
- **Cons:** breaks temporal prediction across the barrier, reducing compression efficiency. It cannot exploit leading pictures for better referencing.

**CRA (Clean Random Access):**

CRA was introduced in HEVC. It is an Intra picture that allows **RASL** (*Random Access Skipped Leading*) frames to reference pictures located before the CRA.

- **Scenario 1, normal playback:** the decoder has past frames in memory. RASL pictures are fully decoded, maximizing compression efficiency.
- **Scenario 2, seeking:** decoding starts directly at the CRA. Past references are missing, so the decoder safely discards, or skips, the RASL pictures and decodes the rest cleanly.
- **Comparison:** H.264 lacked this feature, forcing frequent use of costly IDR frames.

**BLA (Broken Link Access):**

BLA functions similarly to CRA, but explicitly signals that the required reference pictures for its leading frames are destroyed.

- **Origin:** BLA is typically generated by a *secondary encoder* in network nodes, such as transcoders, packagers, and ad-insertion splicers, rather than by the original content encoder.
- **Mechanism:** when a stream is cut and spliced at a CRA, the network node alters the NALU header from CRA to BLA. The decoder reads this and immediately drops the broken RASL pictures without attempting reference lookups.

### Hardware Friendliness: Tiles

- The CTU may be grouped into rectangular regions called **tiles**.
- Tiles are self-contained and independent from slices.
- A slice may contain several tiles, or a tile may contain several slices.
- One of the main targets of tiles is to allow **parallel encoding and decoding** across multi-core processors.
- Another application is viewport priority in 360-degree video.

### Tiles in 360-Degree Video

Tiles are the foundational technology for **Viewport-Adaptive Streaming** in Virtual Reality, especially MPEG-OMAF.

![[Pasted image 20260506140746.png]]

- **The problem:** a VR headset only displays approximately 15% of the full 360-degree sphere, the Field of View. Transmitting the unseen 85% at 8K is a massive waste of bandwidth.
- **The tile solution:** the sphere is projected and encoded as a grid of independent HEVC tiles.
- **Dynamic stitching:** the client requests only the tiles inside the current viewport at high resolution, and requests the background tiles at very low resolution as a fallback.
- **Why tiles?** Motion vectors cannot cross tile boundaries, so tiles are independent and can be mixed and matched at different qualities.

> [!Important] Bandwidth Savings With Tiles
> **Statement:** Tile-based adaptive streaming can reduce bandwidth consumption by 50%-60% without degrading the user's perceived quality.
>
> **Interpretation:** The system spends high bitrate only where the user is looking and sends low-quality fallback content outside the viewport.

### Wavefront Parallel Processing (WPP)

- A finer degree of parallelism can be achieved using **Wavefront Parallel Processing** (WPP).
- Slices are divided into rows of CTUs.
- The encoding of a row may start as soon as two CTUs of the previous row have been encoded.
- There is a guaranteed delay of 2 CTUs between successive rows, preserving spatial prediction contexts.

### Summary of the Hybrid Architecture

**The cost of compression:**

- Standards moved from fixed $16 \times 16$ blocks to extreme QT+MTT geometries up to $128 \times 128$.
- Intra directions expanded from 9 to 67, and sub-pixel affine motion was introduced.
- Complex in-loop filters, such as ALF and Restoration filters, were deployed to mask quantization artifacts.
- NALUs, mappings, tiles, and WPP were engineered to make the compressed video transportable and parallelizable.

> [!Important] The Next Frontier
> **Statement:** The hybrid paradigm has arguably reached its mathematical asymptote with VVC. The next major leap requires abandoning discrete mathematical transforms in favor of end-to-end differentiable neural architectures.
