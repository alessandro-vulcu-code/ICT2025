## Speech and Audio Foundations

### Audio in the Multimedia Ecosystem

*Compression: audio vs. video*

While video accounts for the largest share of network traffic, audio imposes the strictest constraints on the communication chain.

- **The Latency Constraint:** human auditory perception is significantly more sensitive to temporal jitter and delay than the visual system. Real-time interaction in immersive environments requires ultra-low end-to-end latency, below 20 ms.
- **Dynamic Range and Precision:** audio signals span a dynamic range of up to 100 dB. This necessitates high-precision quantization and adaptive strategies to maintain a constant signal-to-noise ratio.
- **Bandwidth vs. Transparency:** the objective is to achieve **perceptual transparency**, where the compressed signal is indistinguishable from the original, at minimal bitrates for high-density network scenarios.

### Methodological Framework

*Synergies with Signal Processing Foundations*

The compression of audio signals integrates and specializes the fundamental coding tools developed throughout the course:

- **Perceptual Thresholding:** moving from general sound perception to specific **psychoacoustic masking models** to optimize the distribution of quantization noise.
- Extending the principles of **predictive quantization** to the physical modeling of the acoustic source, specifically the human vocal tract.
- **Transform Coding:** adapting frequency-domain compaction techniques, such as the DCT, to time-continuous signals via the **MDCT** (*Modified Discrete Cosine Transform*).
- **Entropy Optimization:** applying advanced **arithmetic coding** or **Huffman coding** to reach the information-theoretic limit of the quantized representation.

### Methodological Taxonomy

*Source Modeling vs. Sink Modeling*

Compression strategies are formally categorized based on the specific end of the communication chain they prioritize:

1. **Source-Based Coding (Parametric):** exploits the physical constraints of sound generation. Modeling the vocal tract with **LPC** enables high-intelligibility speech coding at extremely low data rates.
2. **Sink-Based Coding (Perceptual):** exploits the physiological limitations of the human ear. This is the foundation for high-fidelity audio and music, for example AAC and MP3.
3. **Hybrid Architectures:** contemporary standards, such as **Opus** or **EVS**, dynamically integrate both paradigms to handle mixed content, speech and music, with maximal efficiency.

> [!Important] Emerging Frontier
> **Statement:** **Neural codecs** based on generative models are redefining the lower bounds of bitrate by synthesizing high-quality audio from sparse latent representations.
>
> **Interpretation:** Instead of transmitting a precise waveform description, neural approaches transmit compact latent information that a learned decoder uses to generate perceptually plausible audio.

### Temporal Dynamics: Speech vs. Music

*Variability of the Stationarity Assumption*

Predictive and transform coding rely on signal stationarity. Audio exhibits local stationarity, but the duration of this stability window varies drastically across signal types:

- **Speech:** characterized by a quasi-stationarity of approximately 20 ms, dictated by the physical inertia of the human vocal folds and articulators.
- **General Audio/Music:** highly heterogeneous.
  - *Tonal segments*, for example a sustained violin note, can remain stationary for hundreds of milliseconds.
  - *Transients*, for example a drum hit, change their statistical properties in less than 2-5 ms.

> [!Important] Window-Length Compromise
> **Statement:** modern codecs must switch window lengths dynamically to avoid temporal artifacts like pre-echo during transients while maintaining frequency resolution for tonal parts.
>
> **Interpretation:** short windows localize attacks in time, while long windows provide better frequency resolution for stable tonal material.

### Modeling Paradigms

*Source Modeling vs. Sink Modeling*

The choice of compression technique depends on the nature of the signal's redundancy:

- **Source-Based Modeling (Speech):** effective when the generator is known, namely the vocal tract. We use AR filters to model resonances. It is extremely efficient but fails for non-vocal sounds. The priority is *intelligibility* and low latency.
- **Sink-Based Modeling (General Audio):** since music lacks a single physical model, we focus on the receiver, the human auditory system. The priority is *fidelity* and perceptual transparency.

> [!Important] Redundancy vs. Irrelevancy
> **Statement:** speech coding focuses on exploiting **statistical redundancy** through prediction, while audio coding focuses on removing **perceptual irrelevancy** through masking.
>
> **Interpretation:** speech coders model how the signal is produced; perceptual coders model what the listener cannot hear.

### Amplitude Statistics across Audio Types

*PDF Sparsity and Dynamic Range*

The efficiency of optimal quantizers, such as Lloyd-Max quantizers, depends on the shape of the signal's **probability density function (PDF)**:

- **Sparsity:** both speech and many musical instruments exhibit Laplacian-like PDFs, with a sharp peak at zero, indicating that the signal spends most time at low energy.
- **Dynamic Range:** music requires 16-24 bit precision to handle the vast range between silence and peaks, whereas speech is often acceptable with 8-bit non-uniform quantization.

### The Human Speech Production System

*From Anatomy to Signal Processing*

Speech is generated through a highly structured mechanical process that can be mapped directly to a digital signal processing block diagram:

- **The Lungs (Power):** provide the DC air pressure, acting as the system's power supply.
- **The Vocal Folds (Source):** the "sound generator." They vibrate periodically for voiced sounds or remain open for noise-like unvoiced sounds.
- **The Vocal Tract (Filter):** the pharynx, oral cavity, and nasal cavity. These act as an acoustic tube, shaping the sound through resonances.

### Voiced and Unvoiced Speech

*The Two Modes of Acoustic Excitation*

The human vocal system generates two fundamentally different types of acoustic excitation based on the state of the glottis:

- **Voiced Sounds**, for example vowels like /a/ and /u/, or nasals like /m/:
  - **Mechanism:** the vocal folds are held together and vibrate periodically due to airflow.
  - **Signal:** quasi-periodic waveform with a clear **pitch period** $T_0$.
  - **Spectrum:** harmonic structure with energy concentrated at multiples of $F_0 = 1/T_0$.
- **Unvoiced Sounds**, for example fricatives like /s/, /f/, or /sh/:
  - **Mechanism:** the vocal folds remain open, or abducted; turbulence is created by air rushing through a narrow constriction.
  - **Signal:** stochastic, noise-like waveform with no apparent periodicity.
  - **Spectrum:** continuous and "flat" or "colored", depending on the vocal tract shape, similar to white noise.

> [!Important] Voiced/Unvoiced Coding Decision
> **Statement:** the encoder must distinguish between these two states: **voiced** frames require pitch-period estimation, $T_0$, while **unvoiced** frames are modeled using a random noise generator.
>
> **Interpretation:** the same vocal-tract filter can be excited by a periodic impulse train for voiced speech or by noise for unvoiced speech.

### The Source-Filter Acoustic Model

*Decoupling Excitation and Resonance*

Fant's classic theory states that the speech signal $s$ is the convolution of an excitation source $e$ and a time-varying vocal tract filter $h$. In the frequency domain:

$$
S(f) = E(f) \cdot H(f)
$$

Instead of encoding the complex waveform $s$, we aim to transmit the filter parameters that represent the vocal tract and a simplified excitation model: an impulse train for voiced speech and white noise for unvoiced speech.

![[Pasted image 20260513140535.png]]

### Bridge to Linear Predictive Coding

- In the upcoming section, we will learn how to model the $h$ filter by using predictive coding.
- The model is based on a number $P$ of discrete past samples.
- If the prediction model is robust, the residual can be easily modeled either as noise or as an impulse train.
- We work in the discrete domain: the input signal is $x(n)$, and we use the Z-transform to describe the processing architecture.
- The predictor is a discrete linear filter, which turns out to be related to $H$.
- **LPC** means **Linear Predictive Coding**.

## Linear Predictive Coding (LPC)

### Principles of Linear Prediction

*Modeling the Signal and the Residual*

Following the source-filter model, we assume the current signal sample $x(n)$ can be estimated from a linear combination of its $P$ past values:

$$
\hat{x}(n) = -\sum_{i=1}^{P} a_i x(n-i)
$$

The prediction error, or **residual signal** $y(n)$, is defined as:

$$
y(n) = x(n) - \hat{x}(n) = \sum_{i=0}^{P} a_i x(n-i) \quad \text{with } a_0 = 1
$$

> [!Important] Whitening Filter
> **Statement:** the residual $y(n)$ is the output of a FIR filter $A(z) = \sum_{i=0}^{P} a_i z^{-i}$ applied to $x(n)$. If the prediction is optimal, $y(n)$ is a white-noise-like signal or a periodic impulse train.
>
> **Interpretation:** LPC attempts to remove the spectral envelope from speech. The residual should contain mainly the excitation information.

### Estimation of Optimal Coefficients

*Minimization of the Residual Variance*

To find the coefficients $\{a_i\}$, we minimize the variance of the residual $\sigma_y^2 = E\{y^2(n)\}$. As seen in the Predictive Quantization module, setting the partial derivatives $\frac{\partial \sigma_y^2}{\partial a_i} = 0$ leads to the **Yule-Walker equations**:

$$
\mathbf{R}_x \mathbf{a} = -\mathbf{r}_x
$$

where:

- $\mathbf{R}_x$ is a symmetric **Toeplitz matrix** of autocorrelations.
- $\mathbf{r}_x$ is the autocorrelation vector.
- $\mathbf{a} = [a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_P]^T$ is the vector of LP coefficients.

> [!Important] Levinson-Durbin Recursion
> **Statement:** the Toeplitz structure allows the Yule-Walker system to be solved with $\mathcal{O}(P^2)$ complexity.
>
> **Interpretation:** this makes real-time estimation feasible even on low-power devices.

### The LPC Analysis Process

*From Waveform to Parametric Representation*

The encoder segments the signal $x(n)$ into frames, approximately 20 ms long, and extracts the main parameters:

1. **LP Coefficients** $\{a_i\}$: define the spectral envelope, that is, the vocal tract.
2. **Gain** $G$: represents the signal energy, $\sigma_y$.
3. **V/UV Decision:** determines the source type, voiced or unvoiced.
4. **Pitch Period** $T_0$: fundamental frequency, if voiced.

![[Pasted image 20260513140733.png|500]]

### The Autocorrelation Estimation

The short-time autocorrelation $R_x(k)$ is the fundamental tool for extracting both source and filter parameters from a windowed frame $x_w(n)$.

- **Biased Estimator:** as seen in previous modules, we use:

$$
R_x(k) = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1-k} x_w(n) x_w(n+k) \quad \text{for } k = 0, \ldots, K
$$

- **Filter Estimation (Lags 0...P):** these values form $\mathbf{R}_x$ and $\mathbf{r}_x$.
- **Source Analysis (Long Lags):** the same function is used for:
  - **Pitch Detection:** finding the maximum peak at $k > P$.
  - **V/UV Decision:** comparing the peak magnitude against $R_x(0)$.

> [!Important] Autocorrelation Efficiency
> **Statement:** by computing $R_x(k)$ once, the encoder determines the spectral envelope, the signal energy, the periodicity, and the excitation type.
>
> **Interpretation:** one statistic supports both the short-term vocal-tract model and the longer-term pitch analysis.

### Characteristics of the Residual $y(n)$

*What remains after spectral flattening?*

The residual $y(n)$ represents the information that the linear filter cannot capture, namely the excitation source:

- **Voiced segments:** $y(n)$ shows sharp, periodic spikes, corresponding to glottal pulses. The prediction gain is high because the signal is highly redundant.
- **Unvoiced segments:** $y(n)$ resembles zero-mean white noise. The linear model captures the "color" of the turbulence.

> [!Important] LPC Compression Efficiency
> **Statement:** by transmitting the filter $A(z)$ and a simplified model of $y(n)$, we achieve high compression because $y(n)$ has a much lower variance and flatter spectrum than $x(n)$.
>
> **Interpretation:** the decoder reconstructs intelligible speech from a compact vocal-tract model plus a compact excitation model.

### LPC Synthesis

*Reconstructing the Signal via Inverse Filtering*

The synthesis process is the exact mathematical inverse of the analysis. If the prediction error filter $A(z)$ is used to extract the residual $y(n)$ from the signal $x(n)$:

$$
Y(z) = A(z)X(z) \implies X(z) = A^{-1}(z)Y(z)
$$

At the decoder, we reconstruct the speech by applying the **inverse filter** $A^{-1}(z)$ to the excitation:

- **Excitation Source:** $y(n)$ is reconstructed as either a periodic impulse train, for voiced speech, or white noise, for unvoiced speech, based on the received gain, pitch, and V/UV parameters.
- **Time-Domain Implementation:** the synthesis is performed using a linear recurrence equation:

$$
x(n) = y(n) - \sum_{i=1}^{P} a_i x(n-i)
$$

### LPC Spectral Modeling

Since $X(z) = A^{-1}(z)Y(z)$, the general shape of $X$ is determined by $A^{-1}(z)$, because $Y$ is either flat, as in white noise, or a Dirac comb, as referenced in page 18 of the source material.

LPC modeling is more accurate near spectral peaks, the **formants**, than in spectral valleys.

Notice that:

$$
A(z) = a_0 + a_1 z^{-1} + \ldots + a_P z^{-P}
$$

is a FIR filter. Therefore, its inverse is an all-pole IIR filter.

### The Quantization Problem

*Why not quantize $\{a_i\}$ directly?*

To transmit the vocal tract model $A(z)$, we need to quantize the filter coefficients $\{a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_P\}$. However, direct scalar quantization of $\{a_i\}$ is extremely dangerous:

- **High Dynamic Range:** the coefficients $a_i$ can vary wildly in magnitude, making uniform quantization highly inefficient.
- **Lack of Boundedness:** there is no simple relationship to guarantee that the quantized coefficients $\hat{a}_i$ will keep the roots of $A(z)$ inside the unit circle.

> [!Important] Stability Constraint
> **Statement:** if even one pole of the synthesis filter $1/A(z)$ moves outside the unit circle due to quantization noise, the decoder filter becomes unstable, resulting in massive audio distortion.
>
> **Interpretation:** quantization must preserve filter stability, not only coefficient accuracy.

### Line Spectrum Frequencies (LSF)

*The Itakura Solution*

Introduced by Fumitada Itakura in 1975, **Line Spectrum Frequencies (LSFs)** map the LPC polynomial $A(z)$ into two new symmetric and anti-symmetric auxiliary polynomials:

$$
P(z) = A(z) + z^{-(P+1)}A(z^{-1})
$$

$$
Q(z) = A(z) - z^{-(P+1)}A(z^{-1})
$$

This decomposition creates a mathematical "miracle" for quantization.

> [!Important] LSF Property 1: Roots on the Unit Circle
> **Statement:** all roots of $P(z)$ and $Q(z)$ lie exactly on the unit circle. Therefore, they can be represented simply by their angles, or frequencies, $\omega_i \in [0, \pi]$, without needing a magnitude component.
>
> **Interpretation:** converting LPC coefficients to angles gives a better-behaved parameter space for quantization.

### The Interlacing Property

Let $\omega_i^{(P)}$ be the roots of $P(z)$ and $\omega_i^{(Q)}$ be the roots of $Q(z)$.

> [!Important] LSF Property 2: Strict Interlacing
> **Statement:** the synthesis filter $1/A(z)$ is strictly stable if and only if the roots of $P(z)$ and $Q(z)$ interlace, or alternate, along the unit circle:
>
> $$
> 0 < \omega_1^{(P)} < \omega_1^{(Q)} < \omega_2^{(P)} < \omega_2^{(Q)} < \cdots < \pi
> $$
>
> **Interpretation:** stability can be checked and repaired by verifying the order of the LSF angles.

**Why this is perfect for coding:**

- We quantize the frequencies $\omega_i$.
- If quantization error causes two frequencies to swap order, the decoder detects the anomaly instantly.
- The decoder simply re-sorts them to restore strict interlacing, guaranteeing a 100% stable filter.

### From LSFs back to LPC Coefficients

*Reconstructing the Filter at the Decoder*

Once the decoder receives the quantized LSF angles $\{\omega_i\}$, it reconstructs the filter $A(z)$ to perform synthesis:

1. **Form the auxiliary polynomials:** the roots are placed on the unit circle at the received angles:

$$
P(z) = (1 + z^{-1}) \prod_{i=1,3,\ldots}^{P-1} (1 - 2\cos(\omega_i)z^{-1} + z^{-2})
$$

$$
Q(z) = (1 - z^{-1}) \prod_{i=2,4,\ldots}^{P} (1 - 2\cos(\omega_i)z^{-1} + z^{-2})
$$

2. **Reconstruct $A(z)$:** based on the initial definitions, we simply average the two polynomials:

$$
A(z) = \frac{P(z) + Q(z)}{2} \tag{1}
$$

### Visualizing LSFs and Formants

The LSF representation offers a direct visual link between the root positions and the spectral peaks, or formants, of the vocal tract.

1. **Z-Plane (Unit Circle):**
   - Blue roots: $P(z)$.
   - Red roots: $Q(z)$.
2. **Spectral Envelope:** **resonance** occurs when a $P$-root and a $Q$-root are very close together. In that case, the filter $A(z)$ exhibits a sharp spectral peak, namely a formant.

### Efficient Coding: Vector Quantization (VQ)

Instead of quantizing each LSF $\omega_i$ independently, modern codecs use **Vector Quantization (VQ)**.

- **The Concept:** the entire set of $P$ LSFs is treated as a single vector $\omega = [\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_P]$.
- **The Codebook:** encoder and decoder share a "dictionary" of representative LSF vectors, trained on human speech.
- **The Search:** the encoder finds the vector in the codebook that minimizes distortion and transmits only its **index**.

> [!Important] LSF Vector Quantization Advantage
> **Statement:** LSFs are highly correlated because the vocal tract can only take certain physical shapes. VQ exploits this to encode the whole 10th-order filter with just 20-30 bits per frame.
>
> **Interpretation:** vector quantization exploits dependencies among frequencies better than independent scalar quantization.

### LPC-10 Bitrate Breakdown

The LPC-10 standard, Federal Standard 1015, operates at a constant bitrate of 2.4 kbps, or 54 bits per 22.5 ms frame. The bit allocation adapts to the source type:

| Parameter | Voiced (Bits) | Unvoiced (Bits) |
|---|---:|---:|
| LPC Coefficients | 41 (Order $P = 10$) | 20 (Order $P = 4$) |
| Pitch Period ($T_0$) | 7 | 0 (Reserved) |
| Energy (Gain $G$) | 5 | 5 |
| Synchronization | 1 | 1 |
| Error Protection | 0 | 28 |
| **Total Bits / Frame** | **54** | **54** |

> [!Important] LPC-10 Bit Allocation Observations
> **Statement:** voiced frames use maximum spectral detail, $P=10$, and pitch information. Unvoiced frames use lower spectral detail, $P=4$, which leaves significant error-protection bits to improve robustness against channel noise.
>
> **Interpretation:** the encoder spends bits on the parameters that matter for each excitation type.

## Source-Based Codecs

### The CELP Paradigm

*From Open-Loop to Analysis-by-Synthesis*

LPC-10 failed to achieve high quality because the excitation $y(n)$ was too simplistic. **CELP** (*Code Excited Linear Prediction*) introduces three innovations:

1. **Vector Excitation:** the residual $y(n)$ is no longer just pulses or noise, but a vector chosen from a **codebook**, or dictionary.
2. **Analysis-by-Synthesis (AbS):** the encoder acts as a "mini-decoder". It tries different excitation vectors $i$ and different gains $g$, synthesizes the speech, and compares it to the original.
3. **Closed-Loop Search:** the shape and gain of the vector that produces the minimum error are transmitted.

![[Pasted image 20260513133621.png]]

### Perceptual Weighting Theory

*Hiding Noise in the Formants*

The previous scheme is a simplification made to introduce the main concepts of CELP encoders.

To improve the Quality of Experience, CELP does not minimize the physical error $\varepsilon(n) = x(n) - \hat{x}(n)$. It minimizes a **perceptually weighted error**.

- **The Masking Principle:** the human ear is less sensitive to noise in frequency regions where the signal energy is high, namely the *formants*.
- **The Weighting Filter:** CELP uses a filter $W(z)$ to shape the error:

$$
W(z) = \frac{A(z)}{A(z/\gamma)} \quad \text{with } 0 < \gamma < 1 \tag{2}
$$

- **Pole Shifting:** by multiplying the filter coefficients by $\gamma^i$, we move the poles toward the origin, effectively **broadening the formants**.

> [!Important] CELP Perceptual Weighting
> **Statement:** CELP minimizes a perceptually weighted error instead of the raw waveform error, using $W(z) = A(z)/A(z/\gamma)$.
>
> **Interpretation:** the search prefers excitation vectors whose error is hidden under strong speech formants.

- **Red:** the broadened envelope $1/A(z/\gamma)$.
- **Black:** $W(z)$ de-emphasizes the error near the formants, where the signal provides natural masking, and emphasizes it in the spectral valleys.

### The CELP Excitation Model (1/2)

*The Adaptive Codebook and Long-Term Prediction*

The adaptive codebook models the **periodicity**, or pitch, of voiced speech by "recycling" the previous excitation $y(n)$.

- **The Buffer:** a memory of the excitation $y(n)$ synthesized in previous subframes.
- **The Lag $Q$ (Pitch Period):** the adaptive vector is extracted by going back $Q$ samples in the buffer:

$$
y_{\text{adapt}}(n) = y(n - Q)
$$

- **The Gain $b = g_p$ (Pitch Gain):** scales the past excitation to match the current energy.

> [!Important] Long-Term Predictor Interpretation
> **Statement:** the adaptive codebook acts as a **Long-Term Predictor (LTP)**. It effectively implements a filter $B(z) = g_p z^{-Q}$ that models the harmonic structure of the signal.
>
> **Interpretation:** pitch periodicity is represented by reusing delayed excitation rather than by storing a new arbitrary vector.

### The CELP Excitation Model (2/2)

*The Fixed Codebook and Multi-Shape Innovation*

Since the past cannot predict everything, such as transitions or unvoiced noise, a **fixed codebook** provides the "innovation". To achieve high quality without requiring an impossibly large dictionary, the innovation is constructed by combining multiple entries:

- **The Structure:** a static dictionary of shape vectors, for example algebraic pulse patterns.
- **Multi-Shape Combination:** instead of choosing just one vector, the encoder selects **two shape vectors**, $v_1$ and $v_2$.
- **Independent Gains:** each shape vector is scaled by its own gain factor, $g_{c1}$ and $g_{c2}$.

> [!Important] Total CELP Excitation Construction
> **Statement:** the final excitation $y(n)$ sent to the synthesis filter $1/A(z)$ is the sum of the adaptive past and the multi-shape innovation:
>
> $$
> y(n) = \underbrace{g_p \cdot y_{\text{adapt}}(n)}_{\text{Periodic}} + \underbrace{g_{c1} \cdot v_1(n) + g_{c2} \cdot v_2(n)}_{\text{Fixed Innovation}} \tag{3}
> $$
>
> **Interpretation:** the adaptive term represents voiced periodicity, while the fixed-codebook terms represent new unpredictable content.

### Global CELP Architecture

*Implementation in the ITU-T G.729 Standard*
![[Pasted image 20260513134214.png]]

- **Feedback Loop:** the total excitation $y(n)$ is fed back into the adaptive codebook to be used as history for subsequent subframes.
- **Subframe Processing:** the excitation parameters, $Q$, $v_1$, $v_2$, $g_p$, $g_{c1}$, and $g_{c2}$, are updated every 5 ms, or subframe, to capture rapid transients, while the LPC filter $A(z)$ is updated every 10 ms, or frame.

### The Analysis-by-Synthesis Loop

*Filter Memory and the Target Signal ($\hat{p}_0$)*

In the G.729 diagram, before the error is minimized, a signal $\hat{p}_0(n)$ is subtracted from the perceptually weighted input. This is a crucial computational optimization.

- **Filter Components:** the output of the synthesis filter $\frac{1}{A(z/\gamma)}$ is the sum of two responses:
  1. **Zero-Input Response (ZIR):** the "ringing" due to the filter's past memory, namely states from previous subframes.
  2. **Zero-State Response (ZSR):** the response to the *new* excitation, assuming empty memory.
- **The Signal $\hat{p}_0(n)$:** this is exactly the ZIR. It is the output the filter would produce if the current codebook excitation were zero.

> [!Important] Target Signal in Analysis-by-Synthesis
> **Statement:** by subtracting the past memory from the original weighted signal, $\text{Target} = x_{\text{weighted}} - \hat{p}_0$, the codebook search is tremendously simplified.
>
> **Interpretation:** the encoder only needs to match the new codebook vectors, the zero-state response, to this memoryless target signal.

## Perceptual Audio Coding

### Absolute Threshold of Hearing

*Minimum Audible Pressure*

The human ear does not have a flat frequency response. The power required for a sound to be audible, $S_a(f)$, varies significantly with frequency.

- **Audible Range:** typically between 20 Hz and 20 kHz.
- **Maximum Sensitivity:** the threshold is lowest, meaning the ear is most sensitive, between 1 kHz and 4 kHz, which corresponds to the core frequencies of human speech.
- **Coding Implication:** sounds with power $\sigma^2 < S_a(f)$ are not heard and can be discarded by the encoder without any loss in subjective quality.

![[Pasted image 20260513135220.png]]

### Critical Bands (BC)

*The Bark Scale*

The cochlea acts as a bank of overlapping bandpass filters. These filters define the **critical bands**.

- **Definition:** if multiple sinusoids fall within the same critical band, the ear perceives their total power rather than individual components.
- **Variable Width:** the bandwidth of these filters increases with frequency: narrower at low frequencies and wider at high frequencies.
- **Standardization:** AAC uses 51 groups to approximate these critical bands.

![[Pasted image 20260513135313.png|500]]

### Frequency Domain Masking

*Masking Curves $S_m(f_0, \sigma^2, f)$*

A strong "masker" sound reduces the sensitivity to a second "masked" sound at nearby frequencies.

- **Masking Threshold:** for a given frequency $f_0$ and power $\sigma^2$, the threshold $\Phi(f)$ increases, hiding noise below it.
- **Asymmetry:** masking is more effective for frequencies higher than the masker's frequency, $f > f_0$.
- **Coding Gain:** we allow the quantization noise $S_Q(f)$ to increase as long as it remains below the masking curve: $S_Q(f) < \Phi(f)$.

![[Pasted image 20260513135345.png]]

### Temporal Domain Masking

*Pre-masking and Post-masking*

The masking effect also occurs in time, before and after a loud sound.

- **Pre-masking:** lasts about 2-5 ms. The ear is "blinded" just before the onset of a loud sound.
- **Post-masking:** much longer effect, lasting 100-200 ms. Sensitivity recovers slowly after a sound stops.
- **Windowing:** temporal masking justifies the use of specific window shapes to avoid **pre-echo** artifacts in transients.

![[Pasted image 20260513135412.png]]

### Perceptual Coding Standards

*The MPEG Legacy*

The most successful perceptual coders are part of the ISO/IEC MPEG, *Moving Picture Experts Group*, family:

- **MP3:** formally known as **MPEG-1 Audio Layer 3**. It uses a hybrid filterbank, polyphase plus MDCT.
- **AAC:** stands for **Advanced Audio Coding**. It is defined in the **MPEG-2** and **MPEG-4** standards.

**Design Objective:** achieve "transparent" quality, indistinguishable from the original, by removing irrelevant information based on the psychoacoustic model.

### Transform Coding Principles

*Windowing and Overlap*

Perceptual coders operate on discrete blocks of signal called windows.

- **Overlapping:** to avoid blocking artifacts, windows overlap in time.
- **Frame vs. Buffer:**
  - **MP3:** uses a buffer of $N = 512$ samples, coding $M = 32$ new samples.
  - **AAC:** uses a larger buffer of $N = 2048$ samples, coding $M = 1024$ new samples per frame.
- **Window Switching:** modern encoders switch between "long" windows, for steady state, and "short" windows, for transients, to manage temporal masking.

### Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (M-DCT)

*The Engine of Modern Audio Coding*

The **M-DCT** is a block-based transform used to map the signal from the time domain to the frequency domain.

- **Overlap:** it uses overlapping windows, typically 50%, to eliminate blocking artifacts at frame boundaries.
- **TDAC:** *Time Domain Aliasing Cancellation*. The mathematical structure ensures that the aliasing introduced in one block is cancelled by the next during reconstruction.
- **Critical Sampling:** this is a key efficiency property.
  - Despite the 50% overlap, where we process $N = 2M$ samples, the transform produces only $M$ spectral coefficients.
  - **Result:** the number of frequency coefficients equals the number of *new* time samples, meaning there is **no redundancy** or data expansion.

> [!Important] MDCT Critical Sampling
> **Statement:** with 50% overlap, MDCT processes $N = 2M$ samples but produces only $M$ spectral coefficients.
>
> **Interpretation:** overlap improves reconstruction quality and avoids blocking artifacts without increasing the number of transmitted coefficients.

### Generic Perceptual Coder Architecture

*The Analysis-by-Synthesis Loop in Frequency*

The encoder and decoder are asymmetric: most of the complexity lies in the encoder's bit allocation logic.

**Encoder tasks:**

1. Time-frequency mapping.
2. Psychoacoustic analysis.
3. Dynamic bit allocation.
4. Quantization and Huffman coding.

### MPEG-1 Layer 3 (MP3): Signal-to-Mask Ratio

*Signal-to-Mask Ratio (SMR)*

The core efficiency of MP3 comes from its ability to measure the "perceptual headroom" in each subband to guide bit allocation.

> [!Important] Signal-to-Mask Ratio (SMR)
> **Statement:** for a given frequency band $k$, the SMR is the ratio between the signal power $S_k$ and the masking threshold $\Phi_k$:
>
> $$
> SMR_k[\text{dB}] = S_k[\text{dB}] - \Phi_k[\text{dB}]
> $$
>
> **Interpretation:** SMR tells the encoder how much quantization noise can be injected before it becomes audible.

- **Role:** SMR tells the encoder how much noise can be "injected" before it becomes audible.
- **Allocation Goal:** assign enough bits to the subband so that the Signal-to-Noise Ratio, $SNR_k$, exceeds $SMR_k$.
- **Transparency Condition:** when $SNR_k > SMR_k$, the quantization noise $S_Q$ remains below the threshold $\Phi$, becoming **inaudible**.

### MPEG-1 Layer 3 (MP3): Psychoacoustic Model

*Calculating $\Phi(f)$*

To find the global masking threshold $\Phi(f)$, the encoder follows a standardized psychoacoustic procedure:

1. **Spectral Analysis:** a high-resolution FFT is performed to identify the energy distribution.
2. **Identification of Maskers:** the model distinguishes between **tonal** components, such as sinusoids, and **noise-like** components, because they have different masking properties.
3. **Individual Masking Curves:** for each masker, a "spreading function", the masking tent, is calculated based on its intensity and frequency.
4. **Summation:** all individual curves are combined with the **Absolute Threshold of Hearing (ATH)** to form the **Global Masking Threshold** $\Phi(f)$.

### MPEG-1 MP3 vs. MPEG-2/4 AAC

*Evolution of Perceptual Audio Coding*

AAC was designed to be the successor to MP3, fixing its structural limitations and providing better quality at lower bitrates.

| Feature | MP3 | AAC |
|---|---|---|
| **Transform** | Hybrid (Polyphase + MDCT) | Pure MDCT |
| **Frequency Bands** | 32 uniform subbands | **51 Critical Bands (SFB)** |
| **Resolution** | Fixed / Limited | Flexible, up to 1024 bins |
| **Quantization** | Simple / Linear | **Non-linear, Power Law $X^{3/4}$** |
| **Huffman Coding** | Static tables | **Dynamic, 12 different tables** |
| **Typical Bitrate** | Transparent at 192 kbps | **Transparent at 128 kbps** |

> [!Important] Scale Factor Logic in AAC
> **Statement:** unlike MP3's rigid bands, AAC uses the Scale Factor $g$ to surgically place the noise floor for each Scale Factor Band (SFB), ensuring that quantization noise $S_Q$ is always hidden under the masking threshold $\Phi$.
>
> **Interpretation:** AAC has finer and more flexible control over where quantization noise is placed.

### HE-AAC and Unified Codecs

*Spectral Band Replication (SBR)*

Modern standards like **HE-AAC**, or High Efficiency AAC, and **USAC** go beyond simple quantization.

- **HE-AAC:** combines AAC with **SBR** (*Spectral Band Replication*) to replicate high frequencies and with Parametric Stereo.
- **USAC:** a hybrid codec that switches between AMR-WB for speech and HE-AAC for music to handle mixed content seamlessly.

## The Modern Era: Opus and Neural Codecs

### Opus: The Hybrid King

*Blending SILK and CELT*

**Opus** is designed for the modern web, capable of handling everything from low-bitrate VoIP to high-fidelity music with very low latency.

- **SILK Layer:** derived from Skype. Based on **LPC** (*Linear Prediction*), optimized for speech at 8-12 kbps.
- **CELT Layer:** derived from Xiph.org. Based on **MDCT**, optimized for audio and music at high bitrates.
- **The Hybrid Mode:** between 16 and 32 kbps, Opus uses both layers simultaneously, with SILK for the low frequencies and CELT for the highs.

> [!Important] Opus Seamless Adaptation
> **Statement:** Opus can change its bitrate, audio bandwidth, from NB to FB, and coding mode on the fly without any audio glitches or renegotiation.
>
> **Interpretation:** this makes Opus suitable for unstable real-time internet links where bandwidth and packet loss change continuously.

### Opus: Low Latency for the Internet

*Why it replaced MP3 and AAC for Real-Time*

- **Algorithmic Delay:** while MP3 and AAC often require more than 100 ms, Opus can work with frames as small as **5 ms**.
- **VoIP Superiority:** this makes it the standard for WebRTC, Discord, and WhatsApp.
- **Resilience:** includes advanced In-band Forward Error Correction (FEC) to survive packet loss on unstable Wi-Fi and 4G connections.

### The AI Frontier: Neural Codecs

*From Quantization to Generation*

Traditional codecs try to match the *waveform*. Neural codecs, such as Google's **Lyra** or Meta's **EnCodec**, try to match the *perception* using generative AI.

- **Encoder (Feature Extraction):** a neural network compresses audio into a very small latent space, as low as 3 kbps.
- **Decoder (Generative Synthesis):** instead of simple inverse transforms, a **GAN** (*Generative Adversarial Network*) or a **diffusion model** re-synthesizes the voice.
- **Result:** intelligible and natural-sounding speech at bitrates where CELP would sound like broken noise.

> [!Important] Neural Codec Paradigm Shift
> **Statement:** we no longer send the samples; we send "instructions" for the AI to reconstruct a plausible version of the original signal.
>
> **Interpretation:** the codec becomes partly generative, so low bitrate is achieved by relying on learned priors rather than only on waveform coding.

### The Evolution of Spatial Audio

*Channels, Objects, and Scenes*

Modern immersive audio, for VR/AR and cinema, uses three different approaches:

- **Channel-based:** fixed positions, such as 5.1 or 7.1. The mix is baked for a specific speaker layout.
- **Object-based (MPEG-H / Dolby Atmos):** sound sources, for example a "helicopter", are sent as mono signals plus **XYZ metadata**. The receiver renders the sound based on the user's actual speakers.
- **Scene-based (Ambisonics):** captures the entire sound-field sphere. This is ideal for 360 videos and head tracking.

### MPEG-H and Next-Generation TV

*Interactivity and Personalization*

The MPEG-H standard, used in ATSC 3.0 and DVB, introduces user interactivity:

- **Object Control:** the user can manually increase the volume of the "Dialogue Object" or the "Stadium Commentary" while keeping the background noise at the same level.
- **Binaural Rendering:** downmixes 3D audio for standard headphones, simulating a 22.2 speaker setup using **HRTF** (*Head Related Transfer Functions*).

## Conclusion and Evaluation

### Subjective Quality Assessment

*The Gold Standard: MOS and MUSHRA*

The only true judge of audio quality is the human ear. Standardized tests include:

- **MOS (Mean Opinion Score):** listeners rate clips from 1, Bad, to 5, Excellent. A score of 4.0 or higher is usually considered "toll quality".
- **MUSHRA (ITU-R BS.1534):** used for intermediate quality. Listeners compare multiple samples against a "Hidden Reference", the original, and "Anchors", low-pass filtered versions.
- **Transparency Test:** used for high-end codecs such as AAC. The goal is for the listener to be unable to distinguish the original from the compressed version in a double-blind **ABX** test.

### Why SNR is Not Effective for Lossy Audio

*Perceptual Noise vs. Mathematical Noise*

In traditional DSP, we use Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR). In audio coding, this fails because:

- **Masking:** a codec might introduce 20 dB of noise that is perfectly masked by the signal, therefore inaudible. The SNR would be low, but the quality perfect.
- **Phase Shifting:** perceptual codecs often change the phase of the signal. The waveform looks different, with high mathematical error, but it sounds identical.

> [!Important] Shift to Perceptual Metrics
> **Statement:** we need **perceptual metrics** that model the human auditory system to estimate MOS automatically.
>
> **Interpretation:** waveform error is not a reliable proxy for human-perceived audio quality.

### Objective Metrics: POLQA and ViSQOL

*Replacing the Human Listener*

Modern networks use algorithms to predict the MOS score in real time.

- **POLQA (ITU-T P.863):** the industry standard for 4G/5G. It compares the original and degraded signal using a psychoacoustic model, including Bark scale and loudness mapping.
- **ViSQOL (by Google):** a newer metric based on **spectrogram similarity**. It is particularly robust for VoIP and "generative" neural codecs where the time alignment might be imperfect.

### Future Trends in Audio Coding

*The Shift towards AI-Driven and Hybrid Systems*

- **The Neural "Safety Net":** today, AI codecs act as a fallback. If the network bitrate drops below 6 kbps, the system switches from waveform coding to generative reconstruction.
- **Hybridization:** future standards will likely merge **MDCT**, for transparency in critical bands, with **neural layers**, to "imagine" high-frequency textures or background noise.
- **Computation vs. Bandwidth:** a paradigm shift where we use more local battery and CPU power, such as NPUs, to save precious network bandwidth.
- **The "Hallucination" Challenge:** balancing generative realism with mathematical transparency. In professional and legal fields, faithful reconstruction remains superior to AI-generated "plausible" audio.
