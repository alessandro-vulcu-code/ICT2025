## The Hybrid Video Coding Paradigm

### The Universal Hybrid Video Encoder

From MPEG-1 to VVC, every standard utilizes this fundamental architecture:

**Core processes:**
- Spatial (Intra) and temporal (Inter) prediction
- Residual calculation
- Transform and Quantization
- Internal closed loop (Inverse Quantization/Transform) to reconstruct reference frames and prevent *drift*

[Figura: Block diagram of the universal hybrid video encoder. Input frame $\hat{A}$ enters a summation node producing residual $e_B$. Two-stages 4×4 DCT → Quantization → CABAC/CAVLC (output bitstream). In the reconstruction loop: Inv. Quantization → Inverse DCT → produces $\hat{e}_B$ → summation with prediction → Frame Buffer → Deblocking Filter → reconstructed frame $\hat{f}_p$ / $\hat{f}_b$. Mode Decision block selects between Intra Prediction and Motion Compensation. Motion Estimation feeds Motion Compensation from the Frame Buffer.]

### Scope of the Standard

If the fundamental architecture is universal, what exactly is standardized?

[Figura: Diagram showing Source (video) → Pre-processing → Video → Encoding → Bitstream → Channel/Storage. Receiver (video) ← Post-processing ← Video ← Decoding ← Bitstream. Dashed box labeled "Scope of standard" around the Decoding block.]

> **Interoperability and Competition**
> Standards **exclusively** define the bitstream syntax and the decoding process. Encoder architecture and optimization strategies remain open problems for industrial competition.

### Codecs Historical Evolution

To improve compression, standards continuously increase the encoder's "degrees of freedom" (partitioning options, prediction modes).

[Figura: Timeline chart from 1984 to ~2020 showing codec evolution across organizations: CCITT, ITU-T, ISO/IEC, ITU-T/ISO/IEC, On2 Technologies, Xiph.org, Microsoft, Google, Cisco, AOMG. > **The Golden Rule**
> Generational leaps typically halve the required bit-rate for identical perceptual quality, resulting in a computational complexity increase of approximately $10\times$ for the encoder.

### The Cost of Freedom

The empirical data shows that newer standards achieve significantly better PSNR at the same bit-rate, but require ever increasing encoding time.

- **MPEG-2 Underflow:** The encoder reaches its maximum quantization limit. It lacks the tools to discard further bits, resulting in severe coding rate target overshoots (7–8 Mbps).
- **The RDO Challenge:** VVC and AV1 have a massive tree of potential block partitions and prediction modes. Finding the optimal configuration to stay under 2.0 Mbps while maximizing quality requires evaluating millions of combinations.

**How does the encoder make these decisions?**
$\rightarrow$ Rate-Distortion Optimization

## Space Partitioning and RDO

### Recall: Rate-Distortion Optimization

From the previous lecture, we know that the encoder minimizes the Lagrangian cost function for each block:

$$J = D + \lambda R \tag{1}$$

**The Block Partitioning Problem:**
- Partitioning a block into sub-blocks is treated as a distinct coding mode
- The encoder evaluates the cost of the original block ($J_B$) against the sum of the sub-blocks ($J_{split} = \sum J_{subblock}$)
- If $J_{split} < J_B$, the split is accepted
- *The evolution of video standards is largely the evolution of allowable split geometries.*

### H.264/AVC: The Macroblock Era

H.264 relies on a fixed rigid structure, which limits the RDO search space

- **Base Unit:** The $16 \times 16$ Macroblock (MB)
- **Variable Block-Size (VBS):** The MB can be partitioned into $16 \times 8$, $8 \times 16$, or $8 \times 8$ blocks to better capture motion boundaries
- **Sub-partitions:** $8 \times 8$ blocks can be further divided down to $4 \times 4$

> **Limitation**
> A maximum size of $16 \times 16$ is highly inefficient for High Definition (1080p) or 4K video, where smooth background areas (like sky or walls) span hundreds of pixels.

### H.265/HEVC: The Quad-Tree Paradigm

HEVC abandons Macroblocks in favor of the **Coding Tree Unit (CTU)** to handle UHD resolutions

- **Larger Base Size:** CTUs can be up to $64 \times 64$ pixels
- **Quad-Tree Split:** A CTU is recursively split into 4 square Coding Units (CU), down to $4 \times 4$ CUs
- **Functional Separation:** A CU can be symmetrically or asymmetrically divided into Prediction Units (PU) and Transform Units (TU)

[Figura: Left: CTU as single large CU. Right: CTU split into 4 CUs, one of which is further split into 4 sub-CUs. Diagram showing Tree level and Coarser level with PU subdivisions inside CUs for Intra and Inter modes. CU → TU relationship: a CU can contain one large TU, 4 TUs (2×2 grid), or a mixed quad-tree of TUs.]

### H.266/VVC: QT + Multi-Type Tree (MTT)

VVC introduces extreme flexibility, eliminating the strict separation between CUs, PUs, and TUs for most modes

- **Massive Base Size:** CTUs up to $128 \times 128$ pixels
- **QT + MTT:** Quad-Tree leaves can be further partitioned using a Multi-Type Tree
- **New Splits:** Includes *Binary* (symmetric half) and *Ternary* (1:2:1 ratio) splits
- Rectangular blocks become fundamental for encoding object edges accurately.

[Figura: Example of QT+MTT partitioning showing a large block recursively split into a mix of square quad-tree splits, binary splits (horizontal and vertical halves), and ternary splits. Illustrations of binary split (block divided in half) and ternary split (block divided 1:2:1) shown at right.]

### AV1: 10-Way Superblock Partitioning

AV1 utilizes a similar highly flexible hierarchical approach

- **Base Unit:** The *Superblock*, sized up to $128 \times 128$
- **10-Way Split:** A block can be partitioned using 10 different geometrical patterns
- Includes 4:1 and 1:4 rectangular splits, creating highly elongated blocks for sharp directional textures.
- Square blocks can be recursively partitioned

[Figura: Illustration showing a large Superblock that can be split into 10 different partition patterns, ranging from single block to various rectangular and square subdivisions including highly elongated 4:1 and 1:4 ratio blocks.]

### The Algorithmic Burden of RDO

> **The Decision Tree**
> To find the optimal partition, the encoder explores the block geometry using a **Depth-First Search (DFS)** algorithm, optimizing the cost $J = D + \lambda R$.

The evaluation is a massive sequential process:

1. **Top-Down Exploration:** Try coding the block as a whole. Then, try splitting it.
2. **Causal Dependency:** Sub-blocks are tested sequentially (Z-scan order). A block cannot be predicted until top and left blocks are fully *reconstructed* (pixels are frozen).
3. **Inner Loop (Prediction):** For each block, exhaustively test Intra/Inter modes to find its local minimum $J_{CU}$.
4. **Bottom-Up Decision:** Compare the total cost: if $\sum J_{children} < J_{parent}$, the split is kept.

**Conclusion:** While the theoretical state space of a CTU is enormous, reference models traverse it sequentially. Commercial encoders drastically cut this process using **Fast Heuristics and Machine Learning** to prune the tree early.

### Pruning the Decision Tree: Fast Heuristics

To mitigate the extreme complexity of RDO, production encoders (e.g., VVenC, SVT-AV1) implement aggressive early termination strategies.

**Common Pruning Approaches:**
- **Spatial/Texture Analysis:** Evaluating local variance and directional gradients. If a $64 \times 64$ Coding Unit exhibits low variance (e.g., flat background), deeper QT/MTT partitions are immediately bypassed.
- **Temporal Correlation:** Constraining the RDO search space by exploiting the partition depth of the co-located CU in the reference frame.
- **Machine Learning Classifiers:** Deploying lightweight CNNs or gradient boosting decision trees (e.g., LightGBM) to predict split probabilities directly from raw pixel features, discarding highly improbable geometries before any RDO calculation.

## Spatial and Temporal Prediction

### Intra Prediction

Intra prediction exploits spatial redundancy by extrapolating pixels from already-decoded neighboring blocks.

**The Evolution of Freedom:**
- **H.264/AVC:** 4 prediction types for $16 \times 16$ blocks, and 9 types (8 directions + DC) for $4 \times 4$
- **H.265/HEVC:** Expands to 35 modes (33 directions + DC + Planar) to handle complex textures
- **H.266/VVC:** Explodes to 65 directional modes, plus wide-angle modes for non-square blocks

$$P \propto 2^B$$

[Figura: H.264 $16 \times 16$ Intra modes — 2 bits, 4 predictors. Illustration of the prediction block with neighboring reference pixels at top and left. Progressive animation showing Z-scan prediction order across 4×4 sub-blocks within a macroblock.]

[Figura: H.264 $4 \times 4$ Intra modes — 1-4 bits per subblock, $\approx 2 \cdot 10^{15}$ predictors. Direction diagram showing modes 0–8 as arrows radiating from center: 0 (vertical-down), 1 (horizontal), 2 (DC), 3 (diagonal down-left), 4 (diagonal down-right), 5 (vertical-right), 6 (horizontal-down), 7 (vertical-left), 8 (horizontal-up).]

### The Cost of Freedom: Predicting the Predictor

> **The Overhead Problem**
> If VVC has 67 intra modes, sending the mode index requires $\approx 7$ bits per block. For tiny $4 \times 4$ blocks, this signaling overhead would completely destroy the compression gains.

**Solution: Most Probable Mode (MPM)**
- The encoder predicts the mode itself using the spatial context
- A list of Most Probable Modes (MPM) is built using the directions chosen by the Top and Left neighbors
- HEVC uses a 3-candidate MPM list; VVC expands this to a 6-candidate list
- If the chosen mode is in the MPM list, it is coded with a very short index (e.g., 1 or 2 bits)

### Inter Prediction: Fractional Motion Compensation

Inter prediction removes temporal redundancy via Motion Estimation and Compensation (ME/MC)

- Objects rarely move by exact integer pixel amounts between frames.
- **H.264/HEVC/VVC:** Utilize quarter-pel (1/4) fractional precision. The encoder generates sub-pixels using interpolation filters (e.g., 6-tap filters in H.264)
- **AV1:** Pushes the precision even further to 1/8th pixel accuracy, utilizing dynamically selectable interpolation filters (e.g., sharp, smooth)

### Advanced Motion Compensation

Modern standards generalize the concept of temporal distance. A frame is no longer restricted to predicting from its immediate chronological neighbor.

- **Multiple Reference Frames:** Slices can maintain one (P) or two (B) lists of reference images kept in the Decoded Picture Buffer (DPB)
- **Generalized B-Slices:** The encoder can flexibly select predictors from these lists and combine them using weighted averages

[Figura: Illustration of multiple reference frames. Past frames (green, red, blue squares) serve as references for current frame (yellow square). Arrows show motion vectors pointing backward and forward in time from the current frame to multiple reference frames.]

### Vector Prediction: From Skip to Merge

Just like Intra modes, Motion Vectors (MVs) must be predicted to save bits.

- **H.264 (Skip/Direct):** Uses a simple median of spatial neighbors. If the median is correct, 0 bits are spent on MVs
- **HEVC/VVC (Merge Mode):** Replaces the median with a dynamic *candidate list*
  - The list aggregates 5 spatial candidates (adjacent blocks) and 1 temporal candidate (co-located block in the reference frame)
  - The encoder simply transmits the index of the best candidate in the list, sharing both the MV and the reference frame index

### Beyond Translation: Modern Inter Tools

To squeeze the final bits of temporal redundancy out of the signal, AV1 and VVC abandon the assumption that blocks only move rigidly in 2D space.

**Affine Motion Prediction:**
- Allows blocks to rotate, zoom, and shear.
- Uses 4-parameter or 6-parameter models instead of a simple $(x, y)$ vector.

**Compound Prediction:**
- AV1 allows combining Inter and Intra predictions within the same block

[Figura: AV1 Affine Prediction illustration. Left: reference frame showing a green block with control points $(v0, y0)$, $(v1, y1)$. Right: current frame showing the deformed block after affine transformation. Arrow labeled $mv0$ shows the primary motion vector.]

## Transforms, Quantization, and Filters

### Residual Coding: The Evolution of Transforms

After prediction, the residual error must be decorrelated into the frequency domain.

**The push for larger and flexible transforms:**
- **H.264/AVC:** Relies on a $4 \times 4$ integer-coefficient approximation of the DCT, perfectly synchronized to avoid precision errors between encoder and decoder
- **H.265/HEVC:** Expands transforms up to $32 \times 32$ to handle 4K resolutions. It also introduces a DST (Discrete Sine Transform) approximation specifically for $4 \times 4$ Intra blocks, improving compression by $\approx 1\%$
- **H.266/VVC:** Introduces **Multiple Transform Selection (MTS)** and non-square transforms to perfectly match the rectangular partitions created by the QT+MTT structure

### Quantization and Entropy Coding

Quantization is the only lossy step in the hybrid encoder.

**Quantization (QP):**
- In H.264/HEVC, the quantization step size doubles for every increment of 6 in the Quantization Parameter (QP)
- Empirically, a unitary QP increment increases the rate by $\approx 12.5\%$

**Lossless Entropy Coding:**
- Standardized entirely around **CABAC** (Context-Adaptive Binary Arithmetic Coding).
- CABAC transforms syntax elements into binary formats and adapts probabilities dynamically based on previously decoded spatial contexts
- It yields a 5%–15% rate reduction over older variable-length codes (like CAVLC)

### The Need for In-Loop Filtering

Because the transform and quantization are applied independently to disjoint blocks, strong discontinuities (blocking artifacts) appear at block boundaries at low bit-rates.

[Figura: Three images of a football scene: Reference Image (clean), Current Image (slightly degraded), Motion-Compensated (Artifacts) — showing severe blocking artifacts at block boundaries.]

If left uncorrected, these artifacts are saved in the Decoded Picture Buffer (DPB) and will permanently ruin future motion compensations.

### Evolution of In-Loop Filters

To fix quantization damage, standards mandate complex normative filters inside the reconstruction loop.

- **H.264/AVC (Deblocking):** Analyzes edges between $4 \times 4$ blocks. Filtering strength adapts dynamically to the coding mode, motion vectors, and quantization step of neighboring blocks.
- **H.265/HEVC (Deblocking + SAO):** Applies deblocking only on an $8 \times 8$ grid to reduce complexity. Introduces the **Sample Adaptive Offset (SAO)**, which categorizes pixels (edges, bands) and adds offsets to correct ringing artifacts.

### VVC and AV1 Filters

As transform sizes grew and bit-rates plummeted, deblocking and SAO were no longer sufficient. Modern codecs dedicate massive computational power to pixel restoration.

- **H.266/VVC:** Adds the **Adaptive Loop Filter (ALF)** and **Luma Mapping with Chroma Scaling (LMCS)** specifically designed to preserve details in HDR (High Dynamic Range) video.
- **AV1:** Implements a formidable post-processing pipeline:
  - **Directional Filters (CDEF):** Analyzes gradient directions to smooth edges without blurring textures
  - **Loop Restoration Filters:** Wiener filters applied over large windows
  - **Film Grain Synthesis:** Denoises the image before encoding, and synthesizes artificial grain at the decoder to recreate the cinematic look at zero bitrate cost

## Network Abstraction and Parallelism

### Transmission and Network Errors

> **The Fragility of Compression**
> We have compressed a 4K video into a perfect 2 Mbps mathematical stream. But what happens if we transmit this raw bitstream and **just a few bits** are flipped?

**[Play Demo: The Corrupted Bitstream]**

**Different "Pipes", Same Vulnerability:**
- **DVB-T (Broadcast):** Continuous stream subject to electromagnetic noise.
- **Internet (RTP/UDP or TCP/DASH):** Cut into packets (MTU ~1500 bytes) that can arrive late or get lost.
- **Disk Storage (MP4):** Requires specific indexes to seek.

**Consequence:** CABAC loses context. Errors propagate spatially and temporally via Motion Compensation, creating a "green and gray mosaic".

### Pictures and Slices

To limit the error propagation, pictures can be divided into **slices**.

At each new slice, INTRA prediction is stopped and CABAC is reset, such that parallel encoding/decoding of slices is possible.

[Figura: Picture divided into 3 horizontal slices: Slice 0 (top, yellow), Slice 1 (middle, green), Slice 2 (bottom, blue). Slices are shown with staggered boundaries (not strictly horizontal) indicating row-based partitioning with offsets.]

### The Solution: Separation of Concerns

Starting from H.264, video standards separate **compression** from **transport**.

**The Factory Analogy:**

[Figura: Layered architecture diagram. Video sequence → Video Coding Layer (VCL) → Bitstream (H.264) → Network Abstraction Layer (NAL) → NAL Units → Transport Layer → Packets → Network.]

- **Video Coding Layer (VCL):** The production department. It handles pure compression (Transforms, Prediction, RDO). It generates the *slices* of each compressed picture.
- **Network Abstraction Layer (NAL):** The shipping department. It takes VCL slices, encapsulates them, and adds metadata.
- **The Goal:** Provide a standard interface to map the video over RTP/IP (real-time), ISO MP4 (storage), or MPEG-2TS (diffusion).

### NAL Units

The VCL encodes each picture into one or more slices. The NAL encapsulates the slices into **VCL NALUs** (one per slice) and generates **Non-VCL NALUs** (SPS, PPS, SEI).

- **VCL NALU** contains modes, MVs and quantized coefficients of a slice. It can be **Instantaneous Decoding Refresh** (i.e., Intra with CABAC reset) or non-IDR
- **SPS** Sequence parameter set: a non-VCL NALU with important encoding parameters: picture size, color depth, etc.
- **PPS** Picture parameter set: per-picture coding options (specific to each codec)
- **SEI** Supplemental Enhancement Information: not necessary for decoding, but useful for some specific task (e.g., subtitles, HDR info, user data, etc.)
- **AUD** Access Unit delimiter: optional NALU signaling a new picture

### NALUs and Access Units

**Access Unit (AU):** A logical grouping of all NALUs required to decode a single frame. The decoder identifies its boundaries implicitly.

How is this sequence of NALUs actually delivered? If the transport medium is a continuous "blind" tube (e.g., MPEG-TS, raw .264 files), we use the **Byte-Stream format**. If the transport layer already provides boundaries (e.g., an MP4 container), we use the **Packetized format**.

[Figura: Diagram showing Frames (I, B, P, B), Encoded frames (continuous bar), VCL NALU (Slice 1, Slice 2 / Slice 3, Slice 4 / Slice 1, Slice 2 rows for different frames), Non-VCL NALU (SPS, PPS / AUD, SEI for each frame).]

### Format 1: Annex-B (Byte-Stream)

**Mechanism:** A Start Code (e.g., 0x00000001) is injected before every NALU.

**Emulation Prevention:** Fake bytes are injected into the payload to prevent valid video data from accidentally mimicking a Start Code.

**Pro:** Perfect for continuous streaming or broadcasting where packet boundaries do not exist natively.

**Con:** CPU overhead for emulation prevention and re-synchronization. The decoder must scan every single byte sequentially to find the next Start Code.

[Figura: Frames (I, B, P, B) → Encoded frames → Annex-B Byte-stream format single file: rows of NALUs (SPS, PPS, AUD, SEI, Slice 1, Slice 2, Slice 3, Slice 4, AUD, SEI, SEI, PPS, ...) all concatenated into a single binary stream.]

### Format 2: Packetized (Length-Prefixed)

**Mechanism:** A **Length Field** (usually 4 bytes) is written exactly before the NALU header. **No Start Codes:** Emulation prevention is not strictly required for boundary detection.

**Pro:** Highly software-friendly. The parser reads the length and performs an $O(1)$ memory jump directly to the next NALU.

**Con:** Useless if the stream is corrupted or the external container is damaged (loss of synchronization).

[Figura: Frames (I, B, P, B) → Encoded frames → Packetized format: rows with Length field (L) prepended before each NALU (L|SPS, L|PPS, L|AUD, L|SEI, L|Slice 1, L|Slice 2, ...). Multiple rows showing access unit structure.]

### The Evolution: HEVC, VVC, and AV1

**H.265/HEVC and H.266/VVC:**
- Maintain the Annex-B / Packetized dualism.
- The NALU header expands from 1 to **2 bytes** to include *Temporal ID* and *Layer ID* for spatial/temporal scalability.

**AV1: Breaking the Paradigm**
- AV1 abandons the MPEG legacy. NALUs are called **OBUs (Open Bitstream Units)**.
- **No Start Codes:** AV1 completely drops Annex-B and emulation prevention to save CPU cycles.
- It uses exclusively a Length-Prefixed approach even for raw bitstreams.

### Network Friendliness in Action

How the NAL enables intelligent network operations without decoding: The NAL header and structure allow intermediate nodes (MANEs) to understand and manage the video stream.

**1. Router Inspection (MANE):**
- Media Aware Network Elements (MANEs) can read the 1 or 2-byte header: this is a form of *Deep Packet Inspection*
- **Smart Dropping:** If the network is congested, the router can selectively drop Non-Reference B-frames without touching critical IDR frames or Parameter Sets.
- Add protection (CRC) to sensitive data (SPS)

**2. RTP Transport and Fragmentation:**
- If a VCL NALU (e.g., an entire I-frame slice) exceeds the network MTU (1500 bytes), it cannot be transmitted as-is.
- The RTP layer slices large NALUs into **Fragmentation Units (FUs)** and reassembles them at the receiver, keeping the video decoder entirely unaware of the network constraints.

[Figura: Network diagram showing Encoder → Media Aware Network Element (MANE) → Decoder. The MANE reads NALU headers to make forwarding decisions. Stream shows I, B, P, B frames with their NALUs.]

### Resynchronization: Stream Structure

[Figura: GOP structure diagram. Frames in display order: $I_1$, $B_3$, $b_2$, $B_3$ (CRA), $a_4$, $b_6$, $b_8$ (RASL, RASL, RASL), $B_7$, $I_9$, $B_{11}$, $b_{10}$, $b_{12}$, $P_5$, $P_{13}$, *Trailing*. Timeline bar below showing IDR, CRA, RASL RASL RASL positions.]

Decoding may restart at a **Random Access Point (RAP)**:

**IDR (Instantaneous Decoding Refresh):** A strict barrier that completely clears the Decoded Picture Buffer (DPB). No pictures following the IDR in decoding order can reference pictures preceding it.
- **Pros:** Guarantees a clean restart. Ideal for the absolute beginning of a video sequence or hard scene cuts.
- **Cons:** Breaks temporal prediction across the barrier, reducing compression efficiency. It cannot exploit leading pictures for better referencing.

**CRA (Clean Random Access):** Introduced in HEVC. An Intra picture that allows **RASL** (Random Access Skipped Leading) frames to reference pictures located *before* the CRA.
- **Scenario 1 (Normal Playback):** The decoder has past frames in memory. RASL pictures are fully decoded, maximizing compression efficiency.
- **Scenario 2 (Seeking):** Decoding starts directly at the CRA. Past references are missing, so the decoder safely discards (skips) the RASL pictures and decodes the rest cleanly.
- **Comparison:** H.264 lacked this feature, forcing the frequent use of costly IDR frames.

**BLA (Broken Link Access):** Functions similarly to a CRA, but explicitly signals that the required reference pictures for its leading frames are destroyed.
- **Origin:** It is typically generated by a *secondary encoder* in network nodes (e.g., transcoders, packagers, ad-insertion splicers) rather than the original content encoder.
- **Mechanism:** When a stream is cut and spliced at a CRA, the network node alters the NALU header from CRA to BLA. The decoder reads this and immediately drops the broken RASL pictures without attempting reference lookups.

### Hardware Friendliness: Tiles

- The CTU may be grouped into rectangular regions referred to as "tiles".
- The tiles are "self-contained" and independent from slices.
- A slice may contain several tiles, or a tile may contain several slices.
- One of the main targets of tiles is to allow **parallel encoding and decoding** across multi-core processors.
- Another application: viewport priority in 360 video

[Figura: Frame divided into 6 tiles (2 rows × 3 columns): Tile 1, Tile 2, Tile 3 (top row); Tile 4, Tile 5, Tile 6 (bottom row). Each tile contains multiple CTUs.]

### Tiles in 360-Degree Video

Tiles are the foundational technology for **Viewport-Adaptive Streaming** in Virtual Reality (MPEG-OMAF).

[Figura: 32-tile grid (4 rows × 8 columns) representing the equirectangular projection of a 360° sphere. A highlighted viewport region (Tiles 10, 11, 12, 13, 18, 19, 20, 21) is marked as "Viewport (User field of view)". HD Tiles (High Rate) shown in green for viewport; SD Tiles (Low Rate) shown in blue for background.]

- **The Problem:** A VR headset only displays ~15% of the full 360 sphere (Field of View). Transmitting the unseen 85% at 8K is a massive waste of bandwidth.
- **The Tile Solution:** The sphere is projected and encoded as a grid of independent HEVC Tiles.
- **Dynamic Stitching:** The client requests only the Tiles within the current Viewport at High Resolution, and the background Tiles at very Low Resolution (as a fallback).
- **Why Tiles?** Because motion vectors cannot cross Tile boundaries, making them perfectly independent to mix and match qualities.

**Bandwidth Savings:** By using Tile-based adaptive streaming, bandwidth consumption can be reduced by 50-60% without degrading the user's perceived quality.

### Wavefront Parallel Processing (WPP)

- A finer degree of parallelism may be achieved using **Wavefront Parallel Processing (WPP)**.
- Slices are divided into rows of CTUs.
- The encoding of a row may start as soon as two CTUs of the previous row have been encoded.
- There is a guaranteed delay of 2 CTUs between successive rows, maintaining spatial prediction contexts.

### Summary of the Hybrid Architecture

**The cost of compression:**
- We moved from fixed $16 \times 16$ blocks to extreme QT+MTT geometries ($128 \times 128$).
- We expanded Intra directions from 9 to 67 and introduced Sub-Pixel Affine motion.
- We deployed complex in-loop filters (ALF, Restoration) to mask quantization artifacts.
- We engineered NALUs, mappings, Tiles, and WPP to make it transportable and parallelizable.

> **The Next Frontier**
> The hybrid paradigm has arguably reached its mathematical asymptote with VVC. The next major leap requires abandoning discrete mathematical transforms in favor of end-to-end differentiable neural architectures.
