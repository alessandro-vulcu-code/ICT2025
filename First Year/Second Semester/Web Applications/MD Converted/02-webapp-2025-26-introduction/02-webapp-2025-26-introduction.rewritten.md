## Slide 6 - Ted Nelson: Hypertext (1963/1965)

- Theodor Holm Nelson (born 1937) is a philosopher and sociologist, pioneer of ICT.
- He coined the term **hypertext** in 1963, first published in print in an interview at
  Vassar College, NY, in 1965.
- Quote from the source:
  "He introduced the concept of the hyper-text, which would be a more flexible, more
  generalized, non-linear presentation of material on a particular subject."
- Project Xanadu (1960-1998):
  - first hypertext project;
  - released as open source in 2014;
  - transcopyright, original context, bi-directional links, versioning, ...
- References:
  - Nelson, T. H. (1999). Xanalogical Structure, Needed Now More than Ever: Parallel
    Documents, Deep Links to Content, Deep Versioning, and Deep Re-Use. *ACM Computing
    Surveys (CSUR)*, 31(4es):33:1-33:32.
  - Wedeles, L. (1965). Prof. Nelson Talk Analyzes P.R.I.D.E. *Vassar Miscellany News*.
    http://facultysites.vassar.edu/mijoyce/MiscNews_Feb65.html

## Slide 7 - Douglas Engelbart: NLS System (1962-1968)

- Douglas Carl Engelbart (1925-2013), Augmentation Research Center Lab at Stanford
  Research Institute.
- The oN-Line System (NLS) was the first to use the mouse, hyperlinks, windows,
  presentation programs, ...
- The "Mother of All Demos" was presented at the ACM/IEEE Fall Joint Computer
  Conference in 1968.
- Reference:
  Engelbart, D. C. and English, W. K. (1968). A research center for augmenting human
  intellect. In *Proc. December 9-11, 1968, Fall Joint Computer Conference (AFIPS
  1968, Fall, Part I)*, pages 395-410. The Thompson Book Company, Washington, USA.

## Slide 8 - Halasz/Moran/Trigg: NoteCards (1983-1987)

- NoteCards was developed by Xerox in LISP and was one of the most successful and known
  hypertext systems.
- The goal of NoteCards was to support analysts in collecting and organising information
  in order to prepare summary reports.
- NoteCards was extensible through a programming language based on LISP.
- Reference:
  Halasz, F. G., Moran, T. P., and Trigg, R. H. (1987). Notecards in a Nutshell. In
  Carroll, J. M. and Tanner, P. P., editors, *Proc. Conference on Human Factors in
  Computing Systems and Graphics Interface (CHI 1987)*, pages 45-52. ACM Press, New
  York, USA.

## Slide 9 - Tim Berners-Lee: World Wide Web (1989-1991)

- Tim Berners-Lee proposed the Web in 1989 and again in 1990 as a linked information
  system for managing outputs from large projects and experiments at CERN.
- The Web simplified previous research on hypertext and hypermedia systems and was a
  perfect "marriage" with the Internet.
- During 1990, internal development took place at CERN.
- In 1991, the first website was released at CERN: http://info.cern.ch/
- A poster about the WWW was accepted at the 3rd ACM Conference on Hypertext in 1991.
- Reference:
  Berners-Lee, T. (1989). *Information Management: A Proposal*. CERN, Switzerland.
  https://www.w3.org/History/1989/proposal.html

## Slide 19 - The Deep Web

- Raw figures estimate the existence of about 14 billion raw tables, coming from 5.4
  million schemas and comprising more than 5.4 million attributes.
- Gartner puts the relational database market at $26 billion with about 9% annual
  growth, for an expected $40 billion market in 2018.
- The Deep Web alone is estimated to be 500 times the size of the surface or indexable
  Web.
- It was estimated to contain in excess of one billion structured data sets as of
  February 2011.
- It has existed since Web 1.0 and is nowadays confused with the Dark Web.

## Slide 23 - Application Layers

**Presentation/Interface/User Logic**

- Manages the interaction with the user.
- Defines the format and visualisation of information.
- Accepts and validates user input.

**Application/Business Logic**

- Defines and controls the flow of operations in the application.
- Defines the basic operations on the data and their constraints, often called business
  rules.

**Data Logic**

- Manages the persistent storage of the data.
- Searches and retrieves data.
- Ensures consistency of the data.

## Slide 34 - Web Applications

Web applications are an instance of a three-tier architecture.

Diagram elements:

- Database server
- Web + Application server
- Thin client + Browser

Main properties:

- They are based on standard and ubiquitous technologies on the server side, typically
  already part of an organisation's IT infrastructure.
- They do not require any management of the clients, because the client is the browser.
- Clients are ubiquitous and allow access from any kind of device, desktop and mobile.
- Users are already comfortable with the basic interaction patterns and interface
  elements.
