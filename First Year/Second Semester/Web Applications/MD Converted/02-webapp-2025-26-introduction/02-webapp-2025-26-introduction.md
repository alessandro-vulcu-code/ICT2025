## Slide 6 - Ted Nelson: Hypertext (1963/1965)

Ted Nelson: Hypertext (1963/1965)

Theodor Holm Nelson (born 1937) is a
philosopher and sociologist, pioneer of ICT

He coined the term hypertext in 1963, first
published in print in an interview at Vassar
College, NY, in 1965

“He introduced the concept of the hyper-text,
which would be a more flexible, more
generalized, non-linear presentation of
material on a particular subject.”

Project Xanadu (1960—1998), first
hypertext project, released as open source
in 2014

transcopyright, original context, bi-directional
links, versioning, …

Nelson, T. H. (1999). Xanalogical Structure, Needed Now More than Ever: Parallel Documents, Deep Links
to Content, Deep Versioning, and Deep Re-Use. ACM Computing Surveys (CSUR), 31(4es):33:1–33:32.

Wedeles, L. (1965). Prof. Nelson Talk Analyzes P.R.I.D.E. Vassar Miscellany News.

## Slide 9 - Tim Berners-Lee: World Wide Web (1989-1991)

Tim Berners-Lee: World Wide Web (1989-1991)

Tim Berners-Lee proposed (in 1989 and
again in 1990) the Web as an linked
information system for managing outputs
from large projects and experiments at
CERN

The Web simplified previous research on
hypertext/hypermedia systems and was
a perfect “marriage” with the Internet

During 1990 internal development at
CERN

1991 release of the first Web site at
CERN (http://info.cern.ch/)

Poster about WWW accepted at the 3rd
ACM Conference on Hypertext in 1991
