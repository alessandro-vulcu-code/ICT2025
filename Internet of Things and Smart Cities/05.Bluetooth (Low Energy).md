## Bluetooth
### Overview
Bluetooth is a **wireless technology** designed for connecting devices like telephones, computers, cameras, and printers over short distances.  
- Initially proposed by **Ericsson** and standardized as **IEEE 802.15.1**.  
- Created to replace wired connections within computers.  
- Three types of Bluetooth:  
  - **Bluetooth Classic (BR/EDR)**
  - **Bluetooth High Speed**
  - **Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)**

---

## Bluetooth
### Applications
- **Bluetooth Classic (BR/EDR):**  
  - **Audio streaming** (headphones, speakers, car audio systems).  
  - **Peripheral devices** (keyboards, mice, printers).  
  - **File transfers** (sending files between phones, computers, and other devices).
- **Bluetooth High Speed:**  
  - **High-resolution video streaming.**  
  - **Tethering** for internet sharing between devices.
- **Bluetooth Low Energy:**  
  - **Wearables.**  
  - **Beacons** for proximity marketing and location-based services.  
  - **Smart home devices.**

## BR/EDR
### Topology
- Devices connect in small networks called **piconets** (2–8 active devices, up to 255 parked devices).  
- **Master-Slave architecture**: One device acts as the **Master**, and others act as **Slaves**.  
- Master controls the network with a **polling algorithm**.  
- Multiple piconets can combine to form a **scatternet**.

### Frame
The **frame structure** includes:
- **Access code**: Synchronization and piconet ID.  
- **Header**: Contains an 18-bit pattern repeated three times with information such as address (3 bits for 7 destinations), message type, flow control (F), acknowledgment (A), sequence number (S), and header error correction (HEC).  
- **Payload**: Carries data, ranging from 0–2740 bits.

### Layers
Bluetooth communication follows a layered architecture:
- The **PHY layer** deals with the physical transmission of data.  
- The **MAC layer** handles access control and defines data packet formats.

---
### Radio Layer
- Bluetooth operates in the **2.4 GHz ISM band**, divided into **79 channels of 1 MHz each**.  
- Uses **GFSK modulation** (Gaussian Frequency Shift Keying) at 1 Mbit/s.  
- Frequency-Hopping Spread Spectrum (FHSS) is used, with **1600 hops per second**, each frequency being used for only **625 µs** to avoid interference.

### FHSS Coordination
- Bluetooth devices must **synchronize** their frequency hopping sequences during **link establishment**.  
- **Frequency hopping** helps reduce interference, ensuring smooth communication between devices.

### Baseband Layer
- The **Baseband layer** is responsible for organizing the communication between devices, similar to the **MAC layer** in LANs.  
- Bluetooth uses **Time Division Duplex (TDD)** with 625 µs slots.  
- Communication alternates between the **Master** (even slots) and **Slave** (odd slots), with different frequency channels used in each slot.

### Connection Procedure
1. **Inquiry Phase:**  
   - Master initializes communication and establishes the hopping sequence for the piconet.  
   - Devices go into sleep mode.
2. **Paging Phase:**  
   - Master pages a Slave, who responds with a **Device Access Code (DAC)**.  
   - Master sends the list of frequency hops to the Slave, and the Slave replies with a second DAC.

Collision between two slaves is very rare to happen. If it does, he simply ask for a retrasmission.
Odd and even distinction is done because master cannot trasmit and receive at the same time.

### L2CAP
- The **Logical Link Control and Adaptation Protocol (L2CAP)** handles **multiplexing**, **segmentation**, **reassembly**, **QoS** (Quality of Service), and **group management**.  
- Two types of communication:  
  - **Synchronous Connection-Oriented (SCO)**: Low-latency, connection-oriented service.  
  - **Asynchronous Connection-Less (ACL)**: Packet-oriented, error-checked, and asynchronous.
  - If a payload encapsulated in the frame is corrupted, it is retransmitted.

## BR/EDR
### L2CAP ACL Example
- **L2CAP ACL** packets can be 1, 3, or 5 slots long, with each slot occupying a different frequency channel.  
- This reduces overhead by minimizing the need for extra headers and guard times, making data transmission more efficient.
![[Pasted image 20251021091451.png]]

### LMP (Link Manager Protocol)
- The **Link Manager Protocol (LMP)** is responsible for **setting up and managing Baseband connections**.  
- LMP handles authentication, link configuration, and **power management**.  
- It exchanges **Protocol Data Units (PDUs)** between two paired devices to configure settings such as pairing, authentication, and packet size optimization.

---
### HCI (Host Controller Interface)
- The **Host Controller Interface (HCI)** provides a command interface between the **Host** (upper layers) and **Controller** (Baseband, LMP, PHY).  
- It allows the protocol stack to be split between two hardware pieces, connected through a **Host Controller Transport** layer (UART, RS232, or USB).

### SDP (Service Discovery Protocol)
- The **Service Discovery Protocol (SDP)** enables devices to discover services available in the piconet.  
- **Request/response** model is used, where an application requests service information over an **L2CAP connection** and awaits a reply.  
- Service discovery can be done via **searching** (specific service) or **browsing** (all services).

---
### Profiles
- **Profiles** define application-specific protocol stacks in Bluetooth.  
- There are nearly **40 profiles**, each supporting different applications, ranging from narrow uses (e.g., headset profile) to more flexible ones (e.g., **PAN** – Personal Area Network).  
- Networking capabilities depend on the selected profile.

## BLE
### Overview
- **Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)** is a more recent extension of Bluetooth, designed specifically for **low-power IoT devices** and sensors.  
- Key differences from BR/EDR:
  - **Fewer channels**: 40 (2 MHz) vs. 79 (1 MHz).
  - Uses **GFSK modulation** only.  
  - Data rate of up to **2 Mbit/s**, which results in **shorter transmission times** and **lower energy consumption**.  
  - **Adaptive frequency hopping**, with potentially fewer hops.  
  - New error protection codes for more reliable communication.

---

## BLE
### Communication Modes
- BLE nodes can take on **4 roles**, as opposed to just **2 in BR/EDR**:
  - **Broadcaster**: Sends advertisements but does not accept connections (e.g., iBeacon).
  - **Observer**: Listens for advertisements but does not initiate connections.
  - **Peripheral**: Sends advertisements and may accept connections, acting as a **Slave**.
  - **Central**: Can initiate connections and acts as the **Master** after receiving information about a peripheral.

---

## BLE
### New Profiles
- **New BLE profiles** support specialized applications, such as:
  - **Heart Rate Profile**, **Internet Protocol Support Profile**, and the **Mesh Profile**.
- **Mesh Profile** enables **multi-hop communication** in **mesh networks**:
  - **Relay nodes** forward messages between devices.  
  - **Low Power Nodes (LPN)** sleep and wake periodically to exchange data with **Friend Nodes**, which store messages while the LPN is asleep.
  - **Managed flooding** prevents loops in the network.

## BR/EDR vs. BLE
### Comparison
The following table compares the features of **BR/EDR** and **BLE**:

| Feature               | **BR/EDR**                | **BLE**                         |
| --------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Power consumption** | Higher                    | Lower                           |
| **Data rate**         | Up to **3 Mbps** (8-DPSK) | Up to **2 Mbps** (GFSK)         |
| **Latency**           | Lower                     | Higher                          |
| **Connection type**   | Point-to-point            | Point-to-point, broadcast, mesh |
| **Use cases**         | Audio, peripherals        | IoT, wearables, sensors         |
| **Compatibility**     | Classic Bluetooth devices | Bluetooth 4.0+                  |

### Key Differences
- **BR/EDR** is ideal for applications requiring high data rates and low latency, such as audio and video streaming, while **BLE** is optimized for low-power devices such as wearables, sensors, and IoT devices.
