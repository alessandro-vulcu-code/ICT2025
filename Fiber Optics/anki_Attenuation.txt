What is attenuation?	Attenuation, or gradual loss of signal strenght,stands as a critical limiting factor for fiber optic communication systems. In linear regime, losses can be described by a \textit{coefficient of attenuation}. Let $P(z)$ be the power flowing along the fiber. After an infinitersimally long section the power would be reduced by any amount proportional to it, according to: <br>     \[ P(z+\Delta z) = P(z) - \alpha(z)\Delta zP(z) \] <br>     This leads to the fundamental relationship describing power loss: <br>  <br>     \[ \frac{dP}{dz}=-\alpha(z)P(z) \] <br>     Solving this equation yields the general form of the Beer-Lambert Law: <br>  <br>     \[ P(z)=P(0) e^{-\int_{0}^{z}\alpha(z')dz'} \] <br>  <br>     In the common case where the attenuation coefficient ($\alpha$) is constant (linear attenuation), the equation simplifies to its familiar exponential decay form: <br>  <br>     \[ P(z)=P(0) e^{-\alpha z} \] <br>  <br>     Where: <br>     \begin{itemize} <br>         \item $P(z)$: Power at distance $z$ along the fiber. <br>         \item $P(0)$: Initial power at the fiber's start ($z=0$). <br>         \item $\alpha$: Attenuation coefficient (constant). <br>         \item $z$: Distance along the fiber. <br>     \end{itemize}
What are the two types of attenuation?	Intrinsic Attenuation and Extrinsic Attenuation. <br>     Intrinsic Attenuation originates from the fundamental properties of pure silica glass and represents the theoretical minimum loss achievable. It includes: <br>     \begin{itemize} <br>         \item \textbf{Electronic Transitions (UV Absorption)}: High-energy photons excite electronic transitions within silica molecules, causing strong absorption in the UV region. <br>         \item \textbf{Molecular Vibrations (IR Absorption)}: In the infrared region (around 1650 nm), light resonates with the natural vibrational states of silica molecules, leading to strong absorption. <br>         \item \textbf{Rayleigh Scattering}: The most important contribution to loss in the primary transmission windows, caused by microscopic, random fluctuations in the refractive index. Its magnitude has an inverse dependence on the fourth power of the wavelength ($\alpha_{\text{Rayleigh}} \propto 1/\lambda^4$). <br>     \end{itemize} <br>  <br>     Extrinsic Attenuation arises from impurities introduced during the manufacturing process and can be minimized through purification. It includes: <br>     \begin{itemize} <br>         \item \textbf{Hydroxide Ion ($\text{OH}^-$)}: The most significant contaminant, causing strong absorption peaks due to molecular vibrations. Key absorption peaks are around $1.24\ \mu\text{m}$ and $1.38\ \mu\text{m}$. <br>         \item \textbf{Molecular Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$)}: Diffuses into the silica matrix, causing broad attenuation across all transmission, although this process is reversible. <br>         \item \textbf{Bending Losses}: Caused by physical curvature of the fiber, leading to the leak of light into the cladding. Discuss about bending losses.	Bending losses are caused by physical curvature of the fiber, leading to the leak of light into the cladding. This is categorized into: <br>     \begin{itemize} <br>         \item \textbf{Macro-bending losses}: These occur when there is a discernible radius of curvature ($R$). A mode-based model identifies a critical distance ($X_c$) from the fiber's axis where the mode is "locally" at cut-off, causing the light to be radiated. The attenuation coefficient ($\alpha_{\text{bending}}$) has a severe exponential dependence on the bending radius ($R$). <br>         \item \textbf{Micro-bending losses}: This attenuation is caused by a continuous series of small, random bends. The physical mechanism involves the coupling of power from guided modes into random modes. <br>     \end{itemize}
How we describe the trasmission of a signal through a fiber? Frequency and Time domain.	In frequency domain, we have a signal: <br>     \[ \vec{E}(x,y,z,\omega) = A(\omega)\sum_{n=1}^{M}c_n \vec{E}_n(x,y)\exp\left\{-jz\beta_n(\omega)\right\} \] <br>     Since we want to work with the baseband signal, we have to work in $\omega + \omega_0$: <br>     \[ \vec{E}(x,y,z,\omega) = A(\omega)\sum_{n=1}^{M}c_n \vec{E}_n(x,y)\exp\left\{-jz\beta_n(\omega + \omega_0)\right\} \] <br>  <br>     How do we get back to time domain? We have to Taylor-expand $\beta$ around $\omega_0$: <br>     \[ \beta_n(\omega+\omega_0) \approx \beta_n(\omega_0) + \frac{dB_n}{d\omega}\big|_{\omega_0}\omega = \beta_{n,0} + \beta_{0,1}\omega \]
