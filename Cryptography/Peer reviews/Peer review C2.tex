\section*{Peer Review C3 - Problem 6.3}

Given that $2$ is a primitive root modulo $101$, find $x \in \mathbb{Z}/101\mathbb{Z}$ such that $ord_{101}(x)=10$.

---

\section*{Solution}

\textbf{NO AI USED}
We are given that the modulus is the prime number $p=101$.
We are also given that $g=2$ is a primitive root modulo $101$.

The order of the multiplicative group $\mathbb{Z}/101\mathbb{Z}^{\times}$ is $\phi(101) = 101-1 = 100$.
Since $g$ is a primitive root, every element $x \in \mathbb{Z}/101\mathbb{Z}^{\times}$ can be written as a power of $g$, i.e., $x = g^i = 2^i$ for some integer $i$ where $0 \le i < 100$.

The order of an element $g^i$ is given by the formula:
$$ord_{p}(g^{i})=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,i)}$$

In our case, we have $p=101$ and we require $ord_{101}(x)=10$. Substituting the known values:
$$10 = \frac{100}{\gcd(100,i)}$$

This implies that $\gcd(100,i)$ must be $10$:
$$\gcd(100,i) = \frac{100}{10} = 10$$

We seek an integer $i$ such that $\gcd(100, i) = 10$.
Let $i = 10k$ for some integer $k$. Substituting this:
$$\gcd(100, 10k) = 10$$
Factoring out $10$:
$$10 \cdot \gcd(10, k) = 10$$
Dividing by $10$:
$$\gcd(10, k) = 1$$

We have to choose an integer $k$ such that $k$ is coprime to $10$.
The integers that satisfy this condition are $k \in \{1, 3, 7, 9\}$.

Thus, we set $k=1, 3, 7, 9$, which gives the exponents $i$:
$$i_1 = 10 \cdot 1 = 10$$
$$i_2 = 10 \cdot 3 = 30 $$
$$i_3 = 10 \cdot 7 = 70 $$
$$i_4 = 10 \cdot 9 = 90 $$

The elements $x$ we are looking for are $x = 2^i$:
$$x = 2^{10} \pmod{101} \equiv 14 \pmod{101}$$
$$x = 2^{30} \pmod{101} \equiv 17 \pmod{101}$$
$$x = 2^{70} \pmod{101} \equiv 6 \pmod{101}$$
$$x = 2^{90} \pmod{101} \equiv 65 \pmod{101}$$

The required values are $\mathbf{x \in \{6, 14, 17, 65 \}}$.
